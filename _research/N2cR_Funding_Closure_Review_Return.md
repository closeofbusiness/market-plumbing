**Attacks** (numbered, strongest first)

1. [target: conclusion 3] · The programme treats a raw H.8 level change as new credit even though the H.8 notes record five 2025 upward reclassifications into NDFI loans: USD 245.1bn on 1 January, USD 69.4bn on 2 April, USD 69.8bn on 2 July, USD 9.1bn on 1 October, and USD 7.4bn on 31 December, totaling USD 400.8bn ([Federal Reserve Board, *Assets and Liabilities of Commercial Banks in the United States—H.8: Notes on Data*, 28 August 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h8/h8notes.htm)). Subtracting those breaks from the claimed USD 625bn leaves at most about USD 224.2bn before other revisions, repayments, acquisitions, and timing differences; it does not produce an exact organic-origination estimate. · bias: overstates 2025 net new NDFI credit by as much as USD 400.8bn, or 64 percent · settle: H.8 item 1030 break-adjusted growth series and H.8 Notes on Data.

2. [target: A] · Treasury purchases assigned to households are not observed household saving: the Fed says household Treasury transactions are calculated residually from total issuance after measured sectors, and instruments without a published discrepancy often force one sector—usually households—to be the residual lender ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1, Table Descriptions—Instruments*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/html/table_desc_instruments.htm)). An exact adding-up check therefore proves only that the residual closed the identity; it cannot validate the economic identity of the buyer. · bias: unknown sign and potentially the full household row in a period with large source-data revisions · settle: Z.1 Treasury-securities instrument description, household source notes, and instrument-discrepancy table.

3. [target: A] · The label households including hedge funds is not a coherent investor class: domestic hedge-fund assets are usually assigned to the residual household sector, foreign-domiciled hedge funds are assigned to rest of world, and hedge funds are excluded from mutual-fund sectors ([Federal Reserve Board, *Hedge Funds*, 18 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/efa-hedge-funds.htm)). The standalone hedge-fund table has not been incorporated into the core matrix, so the programme's household, foreign, dealer, and repo narratives can describe different legal locations of the same leveraged strategy rather than distinct final savers ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1, Table Descriptions—Supplementary Tables*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/html/table_desc_supplementary.htm)). · bias: misattributes leveraged fund trades to natural-person households and can shift activity across domestic and foreign rows · settle: Z.1 table S124.7.b and the Enhanced Financial Accounts hedge-fund balance sheet.

4. [target: conclusion 1] · The proposed engines are accounting layers, not additive sources of money: a bank loan is the NDFI's liability, the NDFI's Treasury is an asset in A, and repo can finance that same asset again. Ordinary Z.1 tables cannot trace these links; the Fed's experimental FWTW product says core Accounts are not designed to reveal provider-user linkages and that FWTW transaction estimates are still under development ([Federal Reserve Board, *Issuer-to-Holder (From-Whom-to-Whom) Data*, 18 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/fwtw.htm)). · bias: potentially counts one financed Treasury position two or three times as bank creation, NDFI absorption, and repo capacity · settle: transaction-level FWTW data, which are currently unavailable, joined to Call Report borrower and repo counterparty data.

5. [target: conclusion 4] · Net equity issuance and equity buying are opposite sides of different accounts: issuer retirement does not make the issuing corporate sector the buyer of outstanding shares. In the net-purchase rows of F51.1.t, nonfinancial corporate business sold USD 324.2bn in 2024 and USD 197.6bn in 2025, and sold at a USD 197.6bn SAAR in 2026Q1, while households, ETFs, foreigners, and dealers recorded positive purchases ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1, table F51.1.t Corporate equities*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/html/F51_1_t.htm)). · bias: reverses the sign and economic role of the corporate holder sector · settle: Z.1 table F51.1.t, separating net issues lines 1–14 from net purchases lines 15–33.

6. [target: conclusion 4] · The saving-rate identity cannot explain equity valuation: the Fed separates transactions from revaluations and reports that existing equity prices can change household wealth without a purchase of newly issued shares ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1: Recent Developments*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/recent_developments.htm)). Buybacks exchange corporate cash for shares, but neither that transaction nor the accounting identity determines the market-clearing price at which remaining holders value the reduced float. · bias: falsely turns an ex post balance-sheet identity into a valuation mechanism of indeterminate size · settle: Z.1 revaluation table S1M.r alongside F51.1.t transactions and S1M.s levels.

7. [target: A] · The 2026Q1 figures are published at seasonally adjusted annual rates, not as one-quarter cash amounts; the current table shows household Treasury purchases of USD 241.9bn SAAR in Q1, whereas the programme reports about USD 50bn as a quarterly amount ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1, table S1M.t Households and nonprofit organizations*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/html/S1M_t.htm)). A regime claim based on one quarter is especially fragile because the Fed recalculates seasonal factors and warns that stock growth also contains revaluations and other-volume changes ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1: Introductory Text*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/introductory_text.htm)). · bias: fourfold if SAAR and actual-quarter numbers are mixed, plus unquantified seasonal-model error · settle: FA transaction series with explicit SAAR-to-quarter conversion and four-quarter rolling sums.

8. [target: conclusion 3] · The comparison with ABCP is a stock-flow category error: 2025 year-end seasonally adjusted ABCP outstanding was USD 430.5bn, while USD 625bn is presented as a change over a year ([Federal Reserve Board, *Commercial Paper Rates and Outstanding Summary*, 27 August 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/cp/outstanding.htm)). Turnover, maturity, refinancing, and gross issuance make an outstanding stock neither a ceiling on annual financing nor a commensurate benchmark for a loan-flow estimate. · bias: manufactures the bigger-than-the-market superlative; no valid percentage comparison exists · settle: Federal Reserve CP outstanding and issuance/maturity data compared with break-adjusted H.8 transactions over the same interval.

9. [target: conclusion 3] · H.8 item 1030 is funded bank balance-sheet exposure, but it says nothing about whether the NDFI used proceeds for a new final loan, refinanced an existing loan, funded a warehouse, bridged a capital call, or replaced another creditor. FDIC data show that in 2025Q3, 57 percent of funded NDFI loans at reporting banks went to credit intermediaries, 24 percent went to private-equity funds and were mostly capital-call-secured, and another USD 987bn—42.9 percent of total NDFI commitments—was undrawn ([FDIC, *Bank Lending to Nondepository Financial Institutions*, 17 February 2026](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/2026-02/bank-lending-nondepository-financial-institutions)). · bias: equates balance-sheet location and liquidity support with final new credit creation; direction upward, exact amount indeterminate · settle: FFIEC Call Report NDFI subcategories, funded balances, unused commitments, originations, and repayments.

[target: C] · The private-credit subset is far smaller and less utilized than the all-NDFI headline implies: large-bank commitments to BDCs and private-debt funds were about USD 95bn in 2024Q4, of which USD 56bn was utilized, and most exposure was revolving rather than term credit ([Federal Reserve Board, *Bank Lending to Private Credit: Size, Characteristics, and Financial Stability Implications*, 23 May 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/bank-lending-to-private-credit-size-characteristics-and-financial-stability-implications-20250523.html)). The programme silently generalizes from an H.8 bucket that also includes mortgage, business and consumer credit intermediaries, insurers, holding companies, funds, pension funds, and securitization vehicles ([Federal Reserve Board, *Assets and Liabilities of Commercial Banks in the United States—H.8*, 28 August 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h8/current/default.htm)). · bias: overstates funded bank credit specifically feeding private credit or the shadow system by an unquantified but large amount · settle: FR Y-14Q utilized exposure by borrower type and FFIEC item 1030 subcategories.

11. [target: C] · Even the commitments evidence has classification breaks: the May 2026 Financial Stability Report raised the private-equity, BDC, and private-credit category by USD 261bn through vendor-assisted reclassification and put total NBFI commitments at USD 2.6trn ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Stability Report—Leverage in the Financial Sector*, 8 May 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-may-financial-stability-report-leverage.htm)). This demonstrates that a large measured jump can be improved borrower naming rather than funding created in the period. · bias: upward around methodology-change dates; USD 261bn for the cited commitment-category revision, not directly transferable to H.8 funded loans · settle: vintage-consistent FR Y-14Q microdata and revision tables.

12. [target: conclusion 2] · A plateau in sponsored-repo gross volume cannot establish exhaustion of private Treasury demand because repo is secured borrowing and lending turnover, not net acquisition of Treasury securities. BIS finds that the cash-futures basis trade had stagnated since early 2024 but that the interest-rate-swap spread trade drove subsequent growth in hedge-fund repo leverage and Treasury exposure, reaching an inferred upper bound of USD 631bn in 2025Q2 ([Bank for International Settlements, *Sizing up Hedge Funds’ Relative Value Trades in US Treasuries and Interest Rate Swaps*, 8 December 2025](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2512y.htm)). · bias: wrongly infers zero marginal private capacity from one clearing channel while activity migrates across strategies and venues · settle: OFR Form PF exposures by strategy plus bilateral, tri-party, and sponsored repo positions.

13. [target: E] · Flat stablecoin capitalization does not imply flat Treasury buying because reserve composition can rotate among bills, reverse repo, bank claims, and money-market funds without changing tokens outstanding. BIS reports that fiat-backed stablecoins hold those assets in varying proportions and that the net macroeconomic effect of stablecoin demand is ambiguous ([Bank for International Settlements, *Anchoring Trust in Money: Innovation Beyond Stablecoins*, 28 June 2026](https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2026/anchoring-trust-money)). · bias: can miss purchases up to the size of reserve-portfolio reallocation while also double counting bills held through MMFs or repo · settle: issuer-level reserve attestations reconciled to CUSIP holdings and counterparties.

14. [target: A] · Rest of world is a residency-and-custody category, not ultimate nationality or investor type: Treasury says TIC cannot accurately attribute securities held through third-country custodians or foreign portfolio managers ([U.S. Department of the Treasury, *Treasury International Capital Data for June*, 17 August 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0606)). Cayman or U.K. custody can therefore hide U.S.-controlled hedge-fund risk, while the Z.1 assignment of foreign-domiciled funds to rest of world further breaks the programme's foreign-saver interpretation ([Federal Reserve Board, *Hedge Funds*, 18 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/efa-hedge-funds.htm)). · bias: overstates stable foreign final saving and understates leveraged or U.S.-beneficially-owned positions; size cannot be determined from public aggregate data · settle: benchmark TIC surveys combined with nationality-based Form PF and custodial look-through.

15. [target: A] · The residual omnibus row combining state and local governments, GSEs, corporates, and other is not economically interpretable; opposite flows can cancel, and a small net does not mean each channel is dormant. The Z.1 matrix explicitly treats discrepancies as uses of funds and requires sources and uses to add, so closure is a construction requirement rather than an external validation test ([Federal Reserve Board, *Financial Accounts of the United States—Z.1, Table Descriptions—Summaries*, 11 June 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/html/table_desc_summaries.htm)). · bias: suppresses potentially large gross reallocations and launders measurement discrepancy into a reassuring sum check · settle: publish every component, source-vintage, and discrepancy rather than an omnibus remainder.

16. [target: conclusions 2 and 5] · The official defense is monetary-policy-stance neutrality, not balance-sheet neutrality: the New York Fed states that RMPs increase both the Fed balance sheet and reserves, while Jefferson distinguishes short-bill reserve maintenance from QE aimed at lowering long yields and easing broad financial conditions ([Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *The Implementation of Reserve Management Purchases to Maintain Ample Reserves*, 31 March 2026](https://tellerwindow.newyorkfed.org/2026/03/31/the-implementation-of-reserve-management-purchases-to-maintain-ample-reserves/); [Federal Reserve Board, *Economic Outlook and Monetary Policy Implementation*, 16 January 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/jefferson20260116a.htm)). 17. [target: conclusion 1] · The programme's new-money label also ignores liability substitution: through early July the SOMA had purchased nearly USD 250bn of bills, but only about USD 160bn was RMP and USD 90bn was reinvestment of agency-security principal; total Fed assets rose about USD 150bn and reserves only USD 54bn ([Federal Reserve Board, *Monetary Policy Report*, 10 July 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/2026-07-mpr-part2.htm)). 18. [target: F] · Near-zero ON RRP usage is not evidence that a continuing MMF financing engine has disappeared; it means an alternative Fed investment asset was depleted, while MMFs can still fund bills or repo from inflows, maturities, bank deposits, or sales of other assets. The Fed's July 2026 report records ON RRP near zero while MMF and repo allocation remained responsive to relative rates, and the New York Fed describes minimal ON RRP usage as a normal feature of an ample-reserves regime ([Federal Reserve Board, *Monetary Policy Report*, 10 July 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/2026-07-mpr-part2.htm); [Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *Reflections on the Early Days of Reserve Management Purchases and the Maintenance of Ample Reserves*, 26 March 2026](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260326)). · bias: mistakes exhaustion of one portfolio source for exhaustion of the investor's total purchasing capacity · settle: MMF security-level holdings, subscriptions/redemptions, and repo counterparties rather than ON RRP alone.

**Recomputed SOMA-bills / net-bill-issuance ratio (task B)**

- Start numerator: SOMA Treasury bills were USD 233.592bn on Wednesday 31 December 2025 ([Federal Reserve Board, *Factors Affecting Reserve Balances—H.4.1*, 2 January 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/20260102/)).
- End numerator: SOMA Treasury bills were USD 541.995bn on Wednesday 26 August 2026, using the Wednesday level rather than the USD 541.389bn weekly average ([Federal Reserve Board, *Factors Affecting Reserve Balances—H.4.1*, 27 August 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/current/default.htm)).
- Numerator change: USD 541.995bn MINUS USD 233.592bn = **USD 308.403bn**.
- Start denominator: through 31 December 2025, fiscal-year-to-date regular and cash-management bill issues were USD 6,968.419bn and bill redemptions were USD 6,818.204bn, for net issuance of USD 150.215bn ([U.S. Department of the Treasury, *Daily Treasury Statement*, 31 December 2025](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/static-data/published-reports/dts/DailyTreasuryStatement_20251231.pdf)).
- End denominator: through 26 August 2026, fiscal-year-to-date regular and cash-management bill issues were USD 25,145.452bn and bill redemptions were USD 24,301.223bn, for net issuance of USD 844.229bn ([U.S. Department of the Treasury, *Daily Treasury Statement*, 26 August 2026](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/static-data/published-reports/dts/DailyTreasuryStatement_20260826.pdf)).
- Period denominator: USD 844.229bn MINUS USD 150.215bn = **USD 694.014bn** of net bill issuance from after 31 December 2025 through 26 August 2026.
- Recomputed ratio: USD 308.403bn / USD 694.014bn = **44.44 percent**, not 69 percent.

The 44.44 percent is arithmetically date-matched but economically non-causal. The Desk purchases in the secondary market, while the denominator is Treasury face-value issuance less redemptions; the ratio therefore does not show that the Fed bought 44.44 percent of auctions or supplied 44.44 percent of Treasury's cash financing ([Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *Statement Regarding Reserve Management Purchases Operations*, 10 December 2025](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/opolicy/operating_policy_251210a); [U.S. Department of the Treasury, *Daily Treasury Statement*, 26 August 2026](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/static-data/published-reports/dts/DailyTreasuryStatement_20260826.pdf)).

Nor is USD 308.403bn a pure RMP numerator. The Fed reported that nearly USD 250bn of bill purchases through early July comprised about USD 160bn of RMP and USD 90bn of agency-security-principal reinvestment, so H.4.1 holdings alone cannot separate reserve maintenance from portfolio reinvestment through 26 August ([Federal Reserve Board, *Monetary Policy Report*, 10 July 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/2026-07-mpr-part2.htm)).

Applied distinction: RMP is not mechanically balance-sheet-neutral—the New York Fed says it expands assets and reserves—but the official case is that it is **stance-neutral** because it buys short bills to accommodate trend and seasonal liability demand and preserve short-rate control rather than remove duration to depress long yields ([Federal Reserve Bank of New York, *The Implementation of Reserve Management Purchases to Maintain Ample Reserves*, 31 March 2026](https://tellerwindow.newyorkfed.org/2026/03/31/the-implementation-of-reserve-management-purchases-to-maintain-ample-reserves/); [Federal Reserve Board, *Economic Outlook and Monetary Policy Implementation*, 16 January 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/jefferson20260116a.htm)). The ratio survives only as a descriptive change-in-holdings ratio; it does not survive as evidence of monetization without proof of fiscal purpose, primary-market support, yield suppression, or a changed policy stance.
