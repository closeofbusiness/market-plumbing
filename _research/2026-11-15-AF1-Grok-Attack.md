# AF1 v1: Grok attack (Cursor, Grok 4.7 xhigh, fenced read + web, blind rule), 15 Nov 2026

First call timed out at 900 s after fetching six Census documentation pages; the resumed session wrote the review below from them. No sector data was fetched (wrapper fetch log). Rulings are in the spec, Version 2.

The registration cannot answer whether AI-using industries formed more firms. The cross-section is too coarse to separate AI from later sector shocks, and the outcome, the AI measure, the windows, and the labels are each aimed at a different question than the one written in the statements. I did not see or use any Business Formation Statistics (BFS) application counts by sector.

**1. FATAL.** About 15–18 two-digit sectors cannot separate AI use from other post-2019 sector shocks. Remote work, venture funding, interest-rate exposure, the goods-versus-services rotation, and unfinished reopening after 2020 all move the same broad sectors that use more AI. AF1-P2 subtracts only the rank correlation that was already present in 2016–2019 growth. Anything that lined up with AI use only after 2019 stays inside both the SUPPORT and AGAINST labels. The Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) exposure is incumbent employers, not new applicants. There is no amendment that keeps this design as a test of the thesis.

Source: [BTOS methodology (October 2026)](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/methodology/Business_Trends_and_Outlook_Survey_Methodology_V6.pdf) — "target population for BTOS is all employer businesses".

**2. FIXABLE.** High-propensity applications (HBA) are not a common outcome across sectors. Census adds a firm to HBA when its detailed industry is on a list, and that list is not the two-digit sector. Inside professional services the list is only 5411 and 5413, not the rest of sector 54. Manufacturing, construction, retail, education, and health are likewise partial. A shift in mix inside the two-digit bin changes HBA even when total applications do not. Census has also removed internet-sales corporations from HBA on the whole history because they rarely become employers. That is the digital channel the motivating chart is about. HBA is still an application screen, not a count of firms: formations are employer births, and the 2025 formation counts are not finished.

Amendment: label AF1-P1 and AF1-P2 on business applications (BA). Add a second labeled pass on applications with planned wages (WBA). Remove HBA from the labels. Rewrite the question as applications, not new companies. Do not replace the 2025 endpoint with projected formations.

Sources: [BFS methodology](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/technicaldocumentation/methodology.html) — "professional, scientific, and technical services (5411, 5413)"; "internet sales applications were removed from both CBA and HBA"; "low transition rates to employer businesses". [BFS definitions](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/definitions.html) — "formation of an employer (wage-paying) business"; "determined by the most recent quarter". [BFS index](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/index.html) — "updated for the entire time series".

**3. FIXABLE.** The sentence "rose by more than the same sectors did before" is the correlation between AI use and the change in a sector’s growth. The registered statistic is the difference between two correlations. Those are not the same number. A sector can rank high in both periods, so the difference is about zero, while its acceleration is large. The reverse also happens.

Amendment: delete rho(P1) minus rho(2016–2019). The P2 statistic is the Spearman correlation of AI use with g_post minus g_pre, using the growth rates in item 4.

**4. FIXABLE.** The 2022 mean is a reopening base, and it predates the BTOS AI question, which Census added in fall 2023. The 2025 mean includes a broken December. NAICS codes are revised each year for the previous five years; the 12 November 2026 release did that again, so 2022 has been through more revision passes than 2025, and 2019 through more than 2016. How large those revisions are at two-digit level is UNCERTAIN. Census does say the one-time 2022 NAICS backcast was major for information and retail.

Amendment: use not-seasonally-adjusted BA. Set g_post = log(mean of January–November 2025) minus log(mean of January–November 2024). Set g_pre = log(mean of January–November 2019) minus log(mean of January–November 2018). The same eleven months keep December and seasonality out of the comparison. P1 correlates AI with g_post. P2 correlates AI with g_post minus g_pre.

Sources: [BFS index](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/index.html) — "release on November 12, 2026"; "revised each year for the previous five years"; "December 2025 and Weekly data for Week 53, 2025 are impacted". [BFS methodology](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/technicaldocumentation/methodology.html) — "major revisions reflecting content and coding changes in the Retail Trade and Information sectors". [BTOS wording note](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf) — "original BTOS AI question was added to the survey in Fall 2023".

**5. FIXABLE.** The cutoffs are inside ordinary sampling noise. With 15 sectors the null standard error of a Spearman correlation is about 1/sqrt(14) ≈ 0.27, so 0.40 is a t-statistic of about 1.6 on 13 degrees of freedom, short of a one-sided 5 percent test. At 18 sectors it is only on that boundary, and only if no sector is dropped. A difference of 0.30 is smaller than the null standard error of a difference of two correlations (about 0.37 if the two correlations are independent). Independence is UNCERTAIN; persistence would shrink the difference. AGAINST at a correlation of zero or below will happen about half the time when the true correlation is zero, and the text calls that a refutation. Sectors are equally weighted, so a small sector outvotes a large one. Leave-one-out and dropping sectors 51 and 54 are computed and then ignored by the label.

Amendment: draw 10,000 permutations of the AI ranking, holding growth fixed. SUPPORT only if the one-sided permutation p-value is 0.05 or smaller and every leave-one-out correlation is positive. AGAINST only if the opposite tail has p-value 0.05 or smaller. Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE. Delete 0.40, 0.30, and "rho ≤ 0". Add the same rule for a Pearson correlation weighted by each sector’s 2019 mean monthly NSA BA. Register the sample without sectors 51 and 54 as its own statement, with the same permutation rule.

**6. FIXABLE.** Seasonally adjusted industry series are not the sector’s own seasonal adjustment. Census rakes them to the national total, does not adjust utilities, and says raking can push HBA above BA. A full-year sum of those series is still a reconciled series. Matched-month not-seasonally-adjusted sums, as in item 4, avoid that.

Source: [BFS methodology](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/technicaldocumentation/methodology.html) — "industry-level estimates are raked to the United States total"; "except for Utilities (22), is seasonally adjusted"; "forces HBA to be greater than BA".

**7. FIXABLE.** The AI series specified in the registration is not one series. Census broke it after a level shift, and says some respondents did not think "producing goods or services" applied to their firm. The Core Questions file is the new wording. The old wording is a separate original file. The spec names the new filename, the old concept, and a 2024–2025 average. BTOS also did not collect 6 October–16 November 2025. Suppression is an RSE above 50 percent, which falls hardest on low percentages, so a mean of unsuppressed cycles censors low AI readings. Published sector rates exclude firms that operate in more than one sector. BTOS excludes NAICS 525; BFS finance applications are a wider population (how much wider is UNCERTAIN). A new sample is drawn every year inside 2024–2025. The fetched wording note does not call this item question 7 (UNCERTAIN).

Amendment: take AI use only from "AI Core Questions (Original)", and only from periods that end before 17 November 2025. Do not follow CV1-P6 where it crosses the wording break or the shutdown. Keep a sector only if it is published for every period in one pre-declared list. If the 4 December 2023–25 February 2024 full-sample supplement is published by two-digit sector (UNCERTAIN; the methodology I fetched does not say), use that single weighted percentage instead of a cycle mean, and start g_post after February 2024. Drop sector 52 from the labeled sample.

Sources: [BTOS wording note](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/AI%20Question%20Wording%20Updates.pdf) — "Due to a level shift observed in conjunction with the new question wording"; "Core Questions downloads contain the new time series data"; "not see their business as a producer of goods or services". [BTOS methodology](https://www.census.gov/hfp/btos/downloads/methodology/Business_Trends_and_Outlook_Survey_Methodology_V6.pdf) — "no data are available for these panels"; "relative standard errors greater than 50%"; "excluded from sector-level totals"; "Funds, Trusts, and other financial vehicles (NAICS = 525)"; "A new sample is selected every year."

**8. FIXABLE.** National statement AF1-P3 does not measure a post-2020 level shift. Both 2021 and 2022 are already after that shift, the extra 2 percentage points have no basis, and "annualised" is not a formula. The 2025 mean also contains the December outage and, as registered, uses HBA.

Amendment: withdraw the level-shift statement. Replace it with: national mean NSA BA in January–November 2025 is higher than in January–November 2024. SUPPORT if it is higher. AGAINST if it is lower or equal. No 2-point hurdle and no comparison with 2021–22. The statement cannot be read as evidence about AI.

**9. NOTE.** Census backcast 2022 NAICS through the whole monthly history and says the two-digit effect of that one-time switch is minimal. That does not undo the repeated five-year recode in item 4.

Source: [BFS methodology](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/technicaldocumentation/methodology.html) — "impact of implementing the 2022 NAICS vintage is minimal".

**10. NOTE.** The old HBA definition and the 2004–2011 splice sit before the registered years. The 2016 and 2019 endpoints are on the post-2012 definition.

**11. NOTE.** Industry is assigned from the business name. In 2020 that coded over 80 percent of EIN applications. Some applications never receive a code and drop out of every sector series. Whether that share moved between the registered years is UNCERTAIN.

Source: [BFS methodology](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/technicaldocumentation/methodology.html) — "over 80% of all incoming EIN applications in 2020"; "not enough information available to assign a NAICS code".

**Load-bearing assumption.** The 2024–25 ranking of sectors by incumbent AI use is not also the ranking of some other post-2019 shock to applications. Item 3’s pre-period subtraction removes only shocks that already ranked that way in 2016–2019. If reopening, funding, rates, remote work, or the NAICS recode share the AI ranking, both labels are about that shock.
