## What this changes for us

- **Bassman's strongest point agrees with our measurement.** The long-end rise is real yields, not inflation (claims 1–2). Our
  term-premium gap means we cannot confirm his fiscal-trust cause.
- **His weakest point is the one our new data contradicts.** He says the buyers have not changed (claim 5). They have: foreigners'
  share of US absorption collapsed, replaced by money funds and the Fed. His "insurers and pensions are out of gas" (claim 6) is
  partly right: the all-sector total rose, but the liability-matching buyers (private defined-benefit plans, life insurers'
  coupons) were weak, and the 30-year itself we cannot see (C-140).
- **His hyperscaler number is capex, not debt.** 2026 bonds to 13 Oct come to about $229bn, against the $750bn he cites, which
  is the capex forecast (claim 9).
- **Corporations are still large net interest payers** in aggregate (claim 13), so his "rate hikes may not slow the economy"
  argument does not get support from the national accounts.

**The two cheap tests, both run 13 Oct:**
1. **Hyperscaler debt, year to date:** about $229bn (claim 9). Confirm from the Q3 10-Q debt notes in November. Two items are
   open: Alphabet's 8-Ks state C$9.5bn and ¥576.9bn, against tranche sums of C$8.5bn and ¥576.5bn. Separately, the Meta/BlackRock
   El Paso venture's $12.5bn debt is not Meta's own bond and is excluded.
2. **Corporate net interest:** negative, about −$516bn in 2025 (claim 13).

One test the programme already wanted: the long-end buyer by holder, which R2 flagged as unmeasured (claim 6).

## Deep dive, asked for by the principal: the MOVE's mechanics and construction

**Claim A, mechanics.** The MOVE is an annualised implied volatility, and dividing it by about 16 gives the expected daily move;
at about 100 that is roughly 6bp a day across the Treasury complex.
- **Arithmetic: CORRECT.** √252 = 15.87, so 100 ÷ 15.87 = 6.3bp a day as one standard deviation.
- **Units.** The MOVE is a *normal* (basis-point) yield volatility, unlike the VIX, which is a percentage-of-price (lognormal)
  volatility. So "MOVE 100" means ±100bp of yield over a year at one standard deviation.
- **One slip, corrected in the same interview.** He first says 60% of the time; one standard deviation is about 68%, which he
  then says.
- **Applying the index to a single tenor is only approximate.** The MOVE is a weighted average across tenors. The 10-year's own
  volatility differs from the index (he cites 2-years at about 115 and bonds at about 85).

**Claim B, construction:** one-month implied volatility at four points, weighted 2y 20%, 5y 20%, 10y 40%, 30y 20%.
- **Tenors and expiry: CONFIRMED at source.** ICE's release of 16 Oct 2019 (completing its purchase from BofA Merrill Lynch)
  describes one-month OTC options on 2-, 5-, 10- and 30-year Treasuries. Inputs are mid implied volatilities of at-the-money
  options, supplied by ICE Data Derivatives; the close is set at 3pm ET.
- **Weights: NOT in any public ICE document we could open.** The 20/20/40/20 split comes from Bassman, who designed the index at
  Merrill Lynch in 1994, and from secondary sources (an NYU Shanghai thesis, broker explainers). ICE's methodology appears to sit
  behind a login. Grade: creator's statement plus secondary sources, not verified at ICE. *Update, 13 Oct:* Grok (R6) found the
  split in PNC's index catalogue, which reprints ICE's descriptions with permission (the 10-year weighted 40%, the others 20%
  each). The supervisor's download of that PDF failed, so this is relayed, not verified (Grok fetched it in two separate calls, R6
  and K2, with the same sentence).
- **An inconsistency in ICE's own description.** ICE's current developer-catalogue page describes the MOVE as a basket of OTC
  options on US *interest-rate swaps*. The 2019 release says options on Treasuries, with inputs from "bond options and
  swaptions". Whether the underlying moved from Treasury options to swaptions after 2019 is unresolved on public sources. If it
  did, today's MOVE measures swap-rate volatility, and its history is spliced.
- **History.** Bassman says the index dates from 1994, with data back to 1988; a search summary of ICE pages says history from
  1996. This was not resolved by opening a primary page.

**Test C, implied against realized (supervisor's computation, Treasury daily par yields, the same 20/20/40/20 weights).**

*Method: each tenor's daily standard deviation, then the 20/20/40/20 weights applied to those volatilities, as the MOVE weights implied
volatilities. The standard deviation of a weighted yield basket is lower (about 78bp for the last 21 days), because the tenors do
not move perfectly together. Grok (K2) reproduced the 21-day row by the first method.*

- **Implied is above realized.** With the MOVE at about 100, implied volatility sits roughly 17bp above the last month's realized
  83bp and about 30bp above 2026's 71bp. Option buyers are paying a premium for future volatility, which is usual, but here it is
  wide. It is consistent with his reading that 100 is not cheap; in his rule of thumb, 120–130 is a level to sell.
- **The curve's shape matches his.** The front end is more volatile than the long end (2-year realized 97bp against the 30-year's
  70bp over the last month), as he says (2s 115, bonds 85).
- **His rule of thumb, applied.** On 88% of 2026's 195 trading days the 10-year moved by 6.3bp or less, against about 84% if
  2026's realized s.d. were normal. The distribution has slightly heavy tails, as expected.
- **Not verifiable on free data.** The MOVE's own level and history are ICE's proprietary data, not on any free official
  download (E-005). The "about 100" is the podcast's figure, not ours.
