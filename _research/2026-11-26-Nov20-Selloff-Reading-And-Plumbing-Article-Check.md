# 20 November 2026: what moved the market, and a "market plumbing" article checked

26 November 2026. Two things asked on 24 November and answered then in conversation, written down here after an audit
corrected several figures. Grok checked the claims at source twice (24 and 26 November, fenced, read and web only); the
supervisor re-read the Cboe VIX file, Treasury's daily curves, the FOMC statements and the CFTC rows.

## What happened

- **US stocks fell for seven sessions,** from Thursday 12 November to Friday 20 November. On the 20th the S&P 500 fell 3.7%
  to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq Composite 5.28% and the Dow 1.93%.
- **The VIX closed at 35.89** (28.44 the day before; high of the day 40.03). That is the highest close since 11 April 2025
  (Cboe's daily file). It was back to 26.62 on Monday the 23rd.
- **Technology led.** The S&P information-technology sector fell 12.1% on the week. Taiwan's index fell 6.2% on the day and
  17.5% on the week, a record; Korea's and Japan's fell about 5% on the day.
- **Treasury yields fell on the day** (2-year 4.76% to 4.63%, 10-year 5.04% to 4.92%, 10-year real 2.88% to 2.77%).
- **Consumer staples rose** 0.7% on the day.

## The likely driver: a repricing of AI stocks, not a rate scare and not a funding squeeze

The news of 19 and 20 November was about AI companies:
- **Nvidia.** California's attorney general served an investigative subpoena on 19 November. On the 20th Reuters reported
  that SEC staff were examining possible accounting issues (a sourced report; the SEC did not comment). The chief executive
  had resigned on 11 November and was reinstated on the 22nd (8-Ks of 12 and 23 November). The share closed at its lowest in
  a year.
- **Anthropic** listed on 19 November, closed 17.1% above its offer price, and fell about 9% the next day.
- **Policy.** The President said on the 20th that US companies would not be allowed to build data centres abroad.

Three things argue against other explanations:
- **Not a rate scare on the day.** Yields fell. Rate rises were already priced: the FOMC raised to 3.75-4.00% on 16 September
  and held on 28 October with two votes for a further rise; bills yielded 20 to 40bp above the top of the range, which fits
  the near-certain December rise reported that day. Governor Cook's speech on the 20th argued for one.
- **Not a yen-funded unwind, as far as 20 November.** The yen was 158.76 per dollar on 10 November and again on the 20th
  (Federal Reserve H.10). Japan's weekly flows for 8 to 14 November were small.
- **Not a general cut in risk by leveraged funds in futures, in the first week.** They sold the Nasdaq-100 and bought the
  S&P 500 ([first-week note](2026-11-24-Selloff-Positioning-First-Week.md)).

**One amplifier is a candidate, untested:** leveraged funds added a record short in VIX futures in the week to 17 November.
If it had to be bought back on the 19th and 20th, that would add to the move. The pre-registered test is scored on
30 November.

**What this does not show.** Who sold in cash shares, ETFs and options. The futures data sees one corner.

## The article

A Substack article of 20 November ("The Plumbing of a Disconnected Stock Market", Usta Stockman) argues that stocks fell
"in lockstep" with no news because of off-exchange trading, the market-maker exception to the locate rule, and
fails-to-deliver that dilute shareholders. Eleven claims were checked.

| claim | verdict | what the sources say |
|---|---|---|
| About 40-50% of US equity volume trades off-exchange | true | 49.9% in October 2026, 50.6% in 2025 (Cboe) |
| Small and mid-caps routinely print 50-60% off-exchange on volatile days | partly | no series found that shows it |
| Dark pools now process retail-sized trades | misleading | 81% of off-exchange volume is dealers filling orders themselves; 19% is dark pools (Cboe, 2025) |
| Retail buying is absorbed and never lifts the public offer | misleading | a dealer who sells to a retail buyer is short and must buy to hedge; no SEC source describes the one-way story |
| Market makers may short without a locate | partly | only for bona fide market making, and delivery is still required: fails must be closed by T+2 (short sales) or T+4 (Rule 204) |
| Fails can be reset through options or rolled between accounts | misleading | the SEC has charged exactly these schemes as violations (2011, 2012) |
| Fails create phantom supply that dilutes shareholders | misleading | a fail adds no share to the issuer's register (SEC staff guidance) |
| Options volume exceeds share volume; dealers are forced to sell | misleading | true only when dealers are short gamma; market-wide, options are about 3.3bn share-equivalents a day against 17.6bn shares (2025) |
| Volatility-control and risk-parity funds cut exposure when volatility rises | partly | they do; the size is not well measured (ECB, IMF) |
| Transparency is lagged | partly | the lags are real; several 2024 reforms exist or were deferred to 2027-28 |
| Stocks fell in lockstep with no news on 20 November | misleading | the indexes fell by different amounts, staples rose, and the day had the news above |

**Against Michael Green's account.** Green explains unrelated stocks falling together by rule-based flows: index funds that
buy and sell by formula, volatility-control and trend funds that cut exposure as volatility rises, and dealers hedging short
options. In the pages Grok opened he does not use the article's mechanisms (dark pools, the locate exception, fails as
dilution), and he treats same-day options as mostly risk-reducing. The two agree that mechanical flows can move prices
without company news. They differ on which flows.

**Against this project's work.** The project measures flows: who buys equities net, and now futures positions by trader
class. It has not measured off-exchange dealing or settlement fails, so its own data neither confirm nor refute the
article's mechanism; the verdicts above rest on Cboe's and the SEC's published figures and rules. The project also has no
price-impact number of its own (the borrowed multiplier was retracted). The article's reform proposals may have merit on
other grounds; they are not tested here.

## Corrections to what was said on 24 November

- An SEC accounting inquiry at Nvidia **was** reported (Reuters, 20 November). An interim correction saying it was not was
  itself wrong.
- Taiwan's index fell 6.2% on the day and 17.5% on the week, not 5.65% and 14.89%. The technology sector's week was −12.1%,
  not −10.5% (a midday figure). The VIX close was the highest since April 2025, not March.
- "The Fed does not look like it is raising rates" was wrong: it raised in September and a December rise was priced.
- "The Treasury short grew into the sell-off" was one-sided; see the first-week note.
