# AI funding

## Hypothesis

The AI build-out was funded by new money creation, shadow credit, or a broad bond bid. The alternative is that it was funded from operating cash, with the real innovation off balance sheet.

## Status

**Sized.** Mostly self-funded. Hypothesis, re-test on the next 10-Qs and on Oracle’s guarantee maturity. This is a stress case of the equity question, not a separate project.

## Evidence

Three of the five large AI spenders (Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft) generate more operating cash than their capex; NVIDIA, the sixth firm in the funding table, spends little (C-134). Amazon does not (operating cash $161.4bn against capex $173.0bn, twelve months to 30 Jun 2026), and nor does Oracle (operating cash $46.9bn against capex $75.7bn, a gap of $28.8bn; on the funding table's narrower internal cash of $40.8bn the gap is about **−$35bn**, at 31 August 2026). Sources: [`2026-09-12-F1-Funding-Table.md`](../2026-09-12-F1-Funding-Table.md), [`2026-09-12-Oracle-RPO-And-Financing.md`](../2026-09-12-Oracle-RPO-And-Financing.md).

Lease commitments signed but not yet commenced sum to **$1,122.9bn**, about **4.9×** the **$228.3bn** of guarantees contracted. The “13×” figure divided future leases by live guarantees only and was corrected (C-116). Live guarantees were **$86.2bn** at the 22 August reading, which has a known decay date. Source: [`2026-08-22-Guarantee-Stack.md`](../2026-08-22-Guarantee-Stack.md). The $1,122.9bn is the second-quarter reading. Four filers have since reported 30 September figures (Meta $355.7bn, Alphabet $130.5bn, Amazon $164.9bn, Microsoft $364.6bn; [`_research/2026-11-07-Q3-Leases-And-Bonds-Score.md`](../_research/2026-11-07-Q3-Leases-And-Bonds-Score.md)); the six-filer total is restated when NVIDIA and Oracle file.

Oracle remaining performance obligations were **$664bn** at 31 August 2026, against **$455bn** a year earlier, with **$288bn** of additional data-centre lease commitments off balance sheet. Source: the 15 September calendar row and [`2026-09-12-Oracle-RPO-And-Financing.md`](../2026-09-12-Oracle-RPO-And-Financing.md).

**2026 bonds, year to date (13 Oct): about $229bn** of the hyperscalers' own bonds:
- Alphabet $81bn, Amazon $98bn, Meta $25bn, Oracle $25bn, Microsoft none.
- All currencies, at ECB rates on each pricing date; deals from the 8-Ks, listed by Grok.
- Summed to the same dates, the list matches Goldman's $194bn (to 3 August) and JPMorgan's $219bn (to 20 August).

The "$750bn" in the press is a 2026 capex forecast, not borrowing (the BIS, in Bulletin 140, puts the five firms' 2026 capex above $800bn). Source: [`_research/2026-10-13-Grok-Wave-Reviews-Return.md`](../_research/2026-10-13-Grok-Wave-Reviews-Return.md) (R1).

The debt that does exist sits in a corporate bond market held mainly by long money; the AI names are not a separate Z.1 line, and free data count only two holder groups for them (C-139). The hyperscalers were 3% of outstanding US investment-grade debt at end-2025 but over 15% of 2026 issuance by early May (Bank of England, July 2026 FSR), and are close to 5% of the Bloomberg US IG corporate index, 7.5% at 10 years and over (CPRAM, Sep 2026). See the long-money dossier. Amazon’s **$17.5bn** delayed-draw bank term loan, undrawn at 30 June 2026, was drawn in full in September 2026 and outstanding at 30 September (10-Q filed 30 Oct 2026); it matures in September 2029.

**The wider debt measure (24 Nov 2026).** The Fed's November 2026 Financial Stability Report (box 2.2, "Leverage in the Data
Center buildout") puts advanced computing–related debt issuance at more than **$680bn** by October 2026, against $130bn in all of
2023. That is about **31%** of non-financial investment-grade issuance in 2026, and public debt outstanding is above **$1.1trn**.

It is a different and wider borrowing measure than the hyperscalers' own bonds above ($229bn to 13 October; a third-party
tally Grok cited puts SEC-registered bonds at $284bn to 4 November, not rebuilt here). It also takes in loans, project finance and smaller borrowers.

The report also describes a "circular financing" dynamic: hyperscalers and GPU designers lend to smaller firms and take equity
in them. The self-funded finding holds for three of the five spenders (Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft); Amazon and Oracle borrow.
The five's own bonds ($229bn) are about a third of the Fed's issuance figure on a rough comparison (periods and definitions differ); the rest sits around them, which is the open SPV and private-credit leg below. Source: [`_research/2026-11-24-NVIDIA-New-Fed-Webcast-Check.md`](../_research/2026-11-24-NVIDIA-New-Fed-Webcast-Check.md).

## What would change the conclusion

Hyperscaler free cash flow crossing zero and staying there. Further bank borrowing after Amazon's $17.5bn draw in September 2026, or a roll of the maturing guarantees that enlarges the live stack. (The draw itself did not change the status: on trailing twelve months to 30 September the five still summed to +$32.7bn of operating cash after capex, though the four September filers were −$26.4bn on the quarter alone; calendar row 2026-11-06.) A private-credit or SPV leg that the filings do not yet show, large beside the lease stack.

## Data used

SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K) for six firms, hand-read lease notes, XBRL cash and debt where tagged. There is no standing pull script for the lease stack. The next hand-read is the 25 November 2026 calendar row; it waits on NVIDIA's 10-Q, not yet filed on 26 November.
