# Long-money holders

## Hypothesis

The marginal buyer of equity, of duration, and of AI-related debt is long money (insurers, pensions, mutual funds that are not cash pools), as opposed to leveraged money or newly created deposits.

## Status

**Sized for AI-related debt. Open for duration and for commodity paper.** The equity holder table is a separate measurement and is mostly a wrapper-and-residual result. See the passive dossier.

## Evidence

At security level, the AI debt that could be seen in filings is held by long money, not by money funds. Source: [`2026-08-22-D2-Who-Holds-The-AI-Paper.md`](../2026-08-22-D2-Who-Holds-The-AI-Paper.md).

Later composition work (W2): money funds hold about **$4bn**, about **0.35%** of a roughly **$1.2trn** analyst estimate of AI-company-issued debt. The **$1.2trn** is an estimate, not a filing total. Large-bank C&I exposure to AI-adjacent industries is about **$450bn** committed and **$150bn** outstanding, but a similar classification was already about **$250bn** of commitments in 2015, so the build-out is an unmeasured part of that stock (C-115). Source: [`2026-09-22-W2-AI-Paper-Composition.md`](../2026-09-22-W2-AI-Paper-Composition.md).

Pensions were net sellers of the equity instrument over 2024:Q1–2026:Q2 on directly reported lines. That does not say they sold AI bonds.

## What would change the conclusion

A free census that puts a large share of the AI paper in cash pools or in leveraged holders. The next N-PORT pass is on the calendar (around 10 October 2026 for the 2026:Q3 bulk file). A duration holder table on the same basis does not exist yet.

## Data used

N-PORT and 10-K holdings in D2 and W2; Z.1 pension equity lines; money-fund holdings of named paper.
