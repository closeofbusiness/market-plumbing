# N3 v4 — the Singh reconciliation: his numerator, rebuilt from filings
**Date: 2026-08-31. Status: BUILT (+§2b added same day after the Barclays retrieval and the C-071 vintage correction). Supersedes the visual-read basis of D10 §3's ×2.2.**
**Every 2017 figure is filing-sourced and was verified verbatim by me against the fetched 10-K,
not accepted from an agent report.**

## 0. Why this had to be done

D10 compared our measured US bank re-use stock to Singh's Figure 3 (WP/19/106 printed p.14; PDF viewer page 15 — C-072) using **my
visual read of the chart's bars**. That is adequate internally and unusable in a message to the
author of the chart. This note replaces the eyeball with the underlying filings.

It also settles which line to compare. WP/19/106 p.13 defines velocity as *"the ratio of the
total pledged collateral received by the large banks, divided by the primary sources of
collateral"*; Figure 3 is titled *"Pledged Collateral Received by U.S. Banks"* — the bars **are**
the numerator; and p.14 describes the pool as collateral *"that could be onward re-pledged in
their own name"*. So the comparable line is the filings' **permitted-to-repledge** stock, not the
repledged stock. (Convenient, since Citi has never quantified its repledged amount — not in 2017,
not in 2025.)

## 2. Two results beyond the ratio

**Re-use intensified as well as growing.** On the four names that quantify both lines (Citi never
does), repledged/permitted went **80.4% (2017) → 84.0% (Jun-2026)**. The stock more than doubled
*and* a slightly larger share of it is being put to work.

**Our rebuilt panel is CONSISTENT with Singh's own published statement about US share — but does not
independently reproduce it.** ~~independently reproduces~~ **[C-073: the US numerator is ours, from
filings; the *share* divides it by HIS global pool ($7.5trn, ~$13trn). A share computed with his
denominator cannot be independent evidence for his claim about that share. What is independent is the
numerator.]**
His figures: global pledged collateral **$7.5trn at end-2017** (WP/19/106 p.14) → **~$13trn** in
January 2026 (Risk.net), with US banks then accounting for *"over half"*. Our panel implies a US
share of **44.7% (2017) → 57.4% (2026)** — i.e. "over half", derived from filings without
reference to his claim. The global pool grew 1.73× while these five US names grew 2.23×, which is
exactly what a rising US share means.

## 2b. Two independent confirmations that his numerator IS the permitted line — at vintages he actually had

Singh's Risk.net piece (12 Jan 2026) names two banks as "each offering around $1.8 trillion".
Both are checkable, and **the check must use the disclosure vintage available to him on that
date** — a trap I fell into first time and corrected as C-071.

- **JPMorgan.** Latest disclosure available on 12 Jan 2026 = the Q3-2025 10-Q (filed 4 Nov 2025):
  **permitted $1,829.9bn at 30 Sep 2025** (repledged $1,439.9bn). His "around $1.8 trillion"
  **rounds to his stated figure** — 1,829.9 → $1.8trn — and does not match the repledged line ($1,439.9bn). ~~matches to 0.5%~~ [C-072: the percentage gap is **1.7%**, not 0.5%; my arithmetic was wrong. What is true is the rounding, and that the repledged line is nowhere near].
- **Barclays.** Reports in sterling under IFRS 7.15; its FY2025 20-F was filed 10 Feb 2026, i.e.
  *after* his article, so the vintage available to him was FY2024: **£1,318.9bn permitted =
  $1,651bn** at GBP/USD 1.2521 (Fed H.10, 31 Dec 2024). Same broad band; a looser fit than JPM.
  (For the record, FY2025: £1,513.9bn = $2,036bn permitted, £1,382.1bn = $1,858bn repledged, at
  1.3447.) The IFRS taxonomy tags in the FY2025 filing —
  `AssetsHeldAsCollateralPermittedToBeSoldOrRepledgedAtFairValue` and
  `AssetsSoldOrRepledgedAsCollateralAtFairValue` — are documentary confirmation that the IFRS and
  US-GAAP lines are the same disclosure object, not an inference.

**Barclays also gives an independent non-US read on the growth rate:** £608.4bn (FY2017) →
£1,513.9bn (FY2025) = **2.49×** in sterling, against the US five-name panel's 2.23×. The
phenomenon is not US-specific.

**And that raises a question worth carrying:** if the largest US names grew 2.23× and Barclays
2.49×, but Singh's *global* pool grew only 1.73× (7.5 → 13trn), then the rest of his 2017 panel
must have grown far less or shrunk. Credit Suisse no longer exists as an independent filer, and
RBS and Deutsche both deleveraged heavily over the period. **Hypothesis, not a finding** — it
would need the European panel rebuilt, which we have not done.

## 3. What that does to the velocity question — it isolates the denominator

This is the analytical payoff. If our rebuilt **numerator** agrees with his (and the US-share
cross-check says it does), then the disagreement between his velocity ≈2.0 and our mechanism-
signed **1.3–1.5** (N3 §8) cannot live in the numerator. It must live in the **denominator — the
"primary sources of collateral"**.

Our denominator arithmetic, from N3 §8: hedge-fund **repo borrowing $3.24trn (2026Q1) exceeds the
entire Form PF cash-like collateral bucket ($2.92trn)**, so that bucket cannot be mostly true
cash; sizing the only downward wedges generously ($0.88trn: FICC GSD IM $78bn, a $300bn ceiling on
the hedge-fund share of global CCP margin, $500bn of genuine PB cash margin) puts the bracket
floor at 0.73 and hedge-fund sources at **≥$7.3trn**. With the measured real-money leg that gives
velocity ≈ **1.30–1.46**.

**For 2.0 to hold on the same numerator, his source concept must net out roughly $2.5–3trn of
what Form PF grosses up.** That is one question, it is factual, and only he can answer it.

## 4. The question, in its final form

> In the near-2.0 estimate, are hedge-fund "primary sources" taken at **gross** collateral
> posted (Form PF Q43-style, whose cash-like bucket includes Treasuries and agencies), or
> **netted** at fund level — and is the top-20 perimeter still the WP/19/106 list including
> Canadian and Japanese banks?

Secondary, if he engages: does the securities-lending source leg use **on-loan balances**
(ISLA-style) or **beneficial-owner supply**?

## 5. Limits to state plainly if this is shown to anyone

1. **Verb drift across filings.** GS "available to be delivered or repledged"; MS "received with
   right to sell or repledge"; JPM "permitted to be sold or repledged, delivered, or otherwise
   used"; BAC "permitted by contract or practice to sell or repledge"; Citi "may be resold or
   repledged, excluding the impact of allowable netting". Same concept, different words — and
   **only Citi states a netting treatment**, which is itself a comparability gap.
2. **Scope enumerations differ.** MS alone names securities-for-securities and securities-based
   lending; BAC names only a "primary source" and discusses derivatives collateral separately
   without saying whether it sits inside the figure; Citi explicitly includes derivatives.
3. **Mixed dates on the 2026 column** (Citi at Dec-2025) and BAC's one-digit rounding.
4. This is the **US panel only**. Singh's velocity is global; we have not rebuilt his European or
   Canadian/Japanese names, so we cannot compute his global numerator independently.

## 6. The back-test against his Table 2 (3 Sep, completed 4 Sep) — his source series is not a Form PF aggregate

**Read this section, not the first version of it: the one-row conclusion was wrong (C-074).**

His Table 2 (WP/19/106, on disk) publishes sources, pool and velocity for 2007 and 2010–17.
Form PF covers five of those rows. Asking what share of the Form PF cash-like bucket must be
counted to reproduce his hedge-fund source line, year by year:

| year | his HF source ($trn) | our Form PF: securities + other + cash-like ($trn) | implied cash-like share | repo ÷ bucket | his velocity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2013 | 1.85 | 1.37 + 0.03 + 0.63 | **71%** | 0.80 | 2.1 |
| 2014 | 1.90 | 1.52 + 0.04 + 0.69 | **50%** | 0.81 | 2.0 |
| 2015 | 2.00 | 1.49 + 0.06 + 0.71 | **64%** | 0.85 | 1.9 |
| 2016 | 2.10 | 1.46 + 0.06 + 0.86 | **68%** | 0.79 | 1.8 |
| 2017 | 2.20 | 1.82 + 0.07 + 0.93 | **32%** | 0.74 | 2.0 |

**The share is not a convention — it wanders across a 39-point range**, and 2017's 32% is the
outlier. An earlier version of this section reported that 32% as a reconstruction of his method
on the strength of that single row; that is struck (C-074).

### What the drift actually shows

| | 2013 → 2017 | annual steps | sd of steps |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| his hedge-fund source | **+18.9%** | +2.7, +5.3, +5.0, +4.8 | **1.01pp** |
| Form PF collateral posted | **+39.1%** | +10.3, +0.6, +5.3, +19.1 | **6.84pp** |

**His series is smooth; the underlying collateral data is lumpy.** Dividing one by the other is
what produces the wandering share. These are not one object measured two ways — his behaves like
a trended estimate, ours like a measurement. Consistent with his own sourcing line for Table 2:
*"Risk Management Association; Singh (2011); updates to include Canadian banks"* — Form PF is not
among them, and the RMA is a securities-lending data provider.

### What this does to the question for him

It sharpens it, and moves it off conventions entirely:

> Your hedge-fund source series grows about 5% a year with very little year-to-year variation
> (sd of annual steps ~1pp) across 2013–17, while Form PF collateral posted by qualifying hedge
> funds grows +39% over the same years with steps from +0.6% to +19.1%. **Is the hedge-fund
> source line a measured aggregate or a trended estimate — and if measured, from which
> collection?**

That is answerable in two sentences, is not derivable from anything public, and determines
whether his 2.0 and our 1.30–1.46 are two measurements of one quantity or two different
quantities. Nothing else we could ask him is worth as much.

### Caveats
- Form PF covers **qualifying** hedge funds (a reporting-threshold subset); his "hedge funds" may
  be a wider or narrower population, and a population gap alone could explain part of the growth
  difference — though not the smoothness difference.
- The securities-lending leg is untested here; his footnote 13 says it is measured "without
  secondary market churning".
- 2007 and 2010–12 are outside Form PF's history and cannot be tested this way.
