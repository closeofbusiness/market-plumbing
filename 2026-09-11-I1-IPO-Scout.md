### 3. What the prospectuses disclose about buyers (from press coverage, not directly from filing text)
- **Cornerstone investors (named, dollar-denominated, non-binding IOIs):** SK hynix — Baillie Gifford, Coatue, Situational Awareness Partners, combined up to **$7.0bn** (~26% of deal). Medline — 8 named funds (Baillie Gifford, Capital Research, Counterpoint/MSIM, Durable Capital, GIC, Janus Henderson, Viking Global, WCM) up to **$2.35bn** (~37% of deal). Circle — ARK Invest, up to **$150mn**, explicitly sourced to its S-1/A.
- **Directed share programs:** SpaceX reserved up to 5% (~$3.75bn) for employees/executives' friends & family, explicitly **exempt from lock-up**. Cerebras had a similar management/long-tenure-employee DSP with no disclosed dollar figure.
- **Retail allocation:** SpaceX's was unusually large — reported as high as 30%, trimmed to the "low 20s%" before pricing, distributed through Robinhood/Fidelity/Schwab/SoFi/E*TRADE (vs. a typical 5–10%).
- **Indications of interest as demand signal (no named cornerstone):** Cerebras' book was >20x oversubscribed; price range was raised twice before pricing above the top of the revised range.
- **Notably absent:** I found no named cornerstone/anchor tranche for SpaceX itself — a real gap given I couldn't read its 424B4 directly (see unknowns below).

### Caveats (definitions)
- **SPACs** are excluded from Ritter's "net" count entirely: 144 SPAC IPOs raised $26.9bn in 2025 alone — nearly as much as all 90 "net" operating-company IPOs combined ($39.0bn). 2021's SPAC proceeds ($144.5bn) exceed any single year's net-IPO total on record.
- **Foreign issuers/ADRs** are also excluded from "net": SK hynix's $26.5bn ADR listing — the #2 deal of 2025–2026 — would not appear in Ritter's headline number once he publishes 2026 data.
- **Direct listings** raise no new primary capital in the classic case and are tracked separately (Ritter Table 13a); 2022–2026 direct listings have been almost entirely microcap.
- **Unit offerings** (share+warrant) are mostly SPAC-structuring and excluded from "net."
- Ritter's own tables are internally inconsistent by 1 unit in places (e.g., 2019: 113 in Table 1 vs. 112 in Table 15) — he notes this himself.

**Orders of magnitude only, as instructed**: nonfinancial corporations have been net **retiring** far more equity (via buybacks and cash-financed M&A/take-privates) than all IPOs combined raise, in every period except 2020–21. Even 2026's unprecedented IPO haul is unlikely to flip the full-period sign — it's a partial offset, not a reversal, of the underlying trend. This is a genuinely different framing than the brief's proceeds-only ask, and directly relevant to "where the money comes from."
