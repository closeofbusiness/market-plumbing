# SYNTHESIS: Does a financial-circuit / collateral mechanism explain asset inflation without goods inflation?

*All figures below marked [re-derived] were computed by me from primary sources on 2026-08-21, not carried from the lens reports. Where I could not re-derive, I say so.*

---

## 1. THE VERDICT

**Not at all, for equities. Partly and narrowly, for government bonds. And the rival discount-rate account does not rescue the question either — it fails on sign.**

The honest answer is a double negative, and it is more interesting than either affirmative.

**(a) The circuit account fails, and it fails on its own chosen observables — three of them, independently.**

- *Reuse intensity is flat.* Singh's velocity ≈2.0 for a decade; the ECB's transaction-level euro-area reuse rate 11.63% with SD 0.85pp (Jan-2021–Feb-2025); the four US dealers disclosing both legs repledged 79.2% of received collateral in FY2025 against 77.8% in FY2019. What grew was the *quantity* of collateral (six-dealer source collateral +23.9% in 2025), partly as a fair-value remeasurement of the very assets whose inflation is to be explained. The chain got fatter, not longer. A multiplier story needs longer.
- *The micro-channel has been directly tested and returns null.* ECB WP 3147 looked for the haircut wedge by which reuse would generate spendable cash — the "liquidity windfall" — and found it absent on average, often negative, and most negative when collateral moves into central clearing. This is the single best piece of evidence in the corpus and it is negative.
- *Identified credit against securities has SHRUNK relative to the prices it is supposed to explain.* This is new, and it is the finding I would lead with. [re-derived]

**(b) The one series that looks like the circuit — H.8 lending to nondepository financial institutions — is not the circuit.** By the Federal Reserve's own footnote, loans for purchasing or carrying securities, *including margin loans*, are excluded from that line and sit in the adjacent line. The NDFI line is defined as loans to *credit intermediaries* — mortgage, business and consumer credit intermediaries, private equity funds, insurers, securitisation vehicles, hedge funds, pension funds. It is a private-credit and warehouse-funding story, not a buy-equities story. And 28.3% of its seven-year rise is a definitional reclassification with no historical revision. [re-derived]

**(c) But the discount-rate account does not do the work either.** Between 31 Dec 2021 and 19 Aug 2026 the 10-year TIPS yield rose from −1.04% to +2.35%, +339bp [FRED DFII10, re-derived]. Over the same window US nonfinancial corporate equity valuation did not mean-revert: equity to four-quarter-average after-tax profits 27.42 → 29.57 (+7.9%); equity to nonfinancial corporate gross value added 4.277 → 4.276 (flat to three decimals); Tobin's q 1.7007 → 1.8188 (+6.9%), with 2025Q3's 1.9429 the highest of 304 quarters since 1945. [re-derived]. A pure discount-rate model predicts a sharp fall. It did not happen on any of three denominators.

**(d) What actually does the work** is cash flow and its distribution, plus a valuation level that has not reverted. Greenwald–Lettau–Ludvigson attribute 40.2% of the 1989–2017 real equity rise to factor-share reallocation to shareholders against 14.3% to interest rates. Add float retirement (−$1.90trn net nonfinancial equity issuance 2022Q1–2025Q4) and Gabaix–Koijen inelasticity as the amplifier of *any* flow regardless of funding source. The prior synthesis's "discount-rate artefact, not a monetary one" needs qualifying — but the qualification is *"nor a discount-rate one either,"* not *"actually a monetary one."*

**(e) A scope correction the essay must carry.** The premise "financial assets inflate while goods prices do not" is false for 2021–2026: US CPI peaked at 9.1% in June 2022 and core goods are +16.2% since Dec-2019 against roughly flat 1995–2019. The puzzle is real for 2009–2019 — where the collateral circuit ran *backwards* (pledged collateral −39%, velocity −40%, S&P +52%, 2007→2016) — and arguably for 2023–2026. The essay must date its explanandum or a critic will kill it in the first paragraph.

## 2. THE MECHANISM, IF THERE IS ONE

There is one, it is coherent, and it is small. State it this precisely:

**Injection point.** A bank or dealer extends credit *against an existing financial asset as collateral* — Reg-T margin debt, non-purpose securities-based lending, prime-brokerage financing, repo against the position. Not credit to a firm that then invests. Credit whose first and only use is to hold an already-existing claim.

**The circuit.** The loan creates a deposit. The deposit buys the asset from a seller. At settlement the deposit transfers to the seller and stays inside the banking system. No claim is placed on current output, so no goods price is bid. In Keynes's 1937 language this is a *revolving fund of finance*; in Graziani's, initial finance that opens and closes without passing through the market for current output. Because it revolves, the load-bearing observable is **turnover per unit of outstanding balance**, not the balance — which is why every stock metric understates it and why register entry C-015 matters.

**Why the leak should be small.** The buyer never spends into the goods market; the seller has swapped an asset for a deposit and is wealth-neutral, so his marginal propensity to consume out of the swap is near zero.

**Why the leak is in fact not small — and this is fatal.** CBO estimates realised capital gains at **8.7% of GDP in 2021**, the highest in more than 40 years, projected toward 3.7% of GDP long-run. At $32.5trn GDP that is $1.2–2.8trn a year of holding gains converted to cash *in taxable accounts alone*, against roughly $10.5trn/yr of net holding gains — a 12–27% annual realisation rate. Realised gains are income, are taxed as income, and the Treasury collects on them. The claim that circuit money "is never anyone's income" is factually wrong. [CBO publication 58914, Feb 2023 — obtained via search summary; cbo.gov returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch and I did **not** read it at source. Flag as secondary.]

**The observable that would show the mechanism, and what it shows.** Total identified US credit extended against securities, as a share of the equity value it is supposed to be levitating:

**Where the mechanism does survive.** Not as money creation but as *capacity*: DTCC reports ~$1.4trn of balance-sheet capacity released on $3.1trn of sponsored/agent buyside volume (31-Dec-2025), about 45%. Netting relaxes leverage-ratio constraints and permits more gross intermediation per unit of dealer equity. That is real, it is large, and it is a **capacity** mechanism, not a purchasing-power mechanism — and it shows up as a *shrinking* Singh numerator, not a rising velocity. And per Singh's own sanctioned route (the pledgeability premium, WP/21/94), the price effect lands on **pledgeable assets — government bonds** — i.e. it lowers their yield. It is a discount-rate mechanism in collateral clothing, and it does not reach equities.

## 3. WHAT SINGH ACTUALLY CONTRIBUTES

**What the project hoped:** that collateral reuse is a money-multiplier for asset prices.

**What Singh actually claims:** pledged collateral is a money-substitute whose circulation determines how much *secured lending* a given stock of securities can support. In his formal statement it enters the **LM curve** — M = f(Y, r, cp), WP/21/6 Annex — where the dependent variable is **output and monetary transmission**. The strings "asset price", "equity price" and "stock price" appear **zero times** across the five core collateral papers (WP/10/172, 11/256, 12/179, 17/113, 19/106). His argument is a reason QE did **less** than the balance sheet suggests, not more. And in the ZIRP window the operator is asking about, his measured velocity *fell* from 3.0 (2007) to 1.8 (2016) while the S&P rose 52%. His corpus does not carry the operator's mechanism, and its historical direction runs the wrong way.

His silence is evidence about Singh, not about the world. Nobody in this literature has tested an asset-price channel. "Undermines" means the corpus does not support the thesis, not that the channel has been shown absent.

## 4. THE DISCRIMINATING TEST

**The test is a law-of-one-price violation across financeability, measured around a dated, rate-orthogonal shock to financing capacity.**

The two accounts differ in exactly one testable way. A discount-rate account is arbitrage-enforced: two claims to identical cash flows must trade at the same price, whatever their collateral value. A circuit account *requires* a wedge — purchasing power is created for a specific set of assets by a specific set of lenders against a specific set of collateral — and requires that wedge to move with financing capacity rather than with rates.

**The shock is available, dated, quantified, and it is happening now.** On **25 November 2025** the Federal Reserve (5–2), FDIC and OCC finalised the eSLR recalibration; Federal Register 1 December 2025 (2025-21626). The depository-institution standard falls from 6% (3% SLR + 3% buffer) to 3% plus the lesser of 1% or 50% of the method-1 G-SIB surcharge. **Effective 1 April 2026, early adoption from 1 January 2026.** Estimated reduction in aggregate holding-company tier 1 ≈ **$13bn, just under 2%**, with the OCC noting *"certain depository institution subsidiaries could see greater reductions."* This is a pure expansion of dealer balance-sheet capacity with no change in policy rates, cash flows, or the discount rate — and it lands inside the operator's window.

**The observation to make:**
1. *Bond leg (Singh-sanctioned, and where the effect should be largest):* Treasury cash-futures basis, swap spreads, and the GC-to-specials spread across the 25-Nov-2025 announcement, the 1-Jan-2026 early-adoption date and the 1-Apr-2026 effective date.
2. *Equity leg (the one that matters for the operator's phenomenon, and which nobody has run):* the S&P 500 futures- and TRS-implied financing spread over OIS, against the index level and against the valuation multiple, around the same three dates. The funding spread *is* the observable price of equity balance-sheet capacity.

**Predictions.** Circuit: capacity relief → cheaper and wider financing → higher prices of financed assets, with no rate move. Discount rate: capacity relief moves the *basis* — a financing wedge arbitrage confines to the spread — and leaves the level untouched.

**Why this test has teeth rather than being another co-movement exercise:** the shock has a dollar magnitude, so it yields an elasticity rather than a sign; and the falsifying outcome is specified in advance.

**One prior that should temper expectations.** The most financeable equity wrapper available — an S&P 500 ETF share, which is easier to margin and lend than the basket — trades within a basis point or two of NAV. If financeability carried a valuation premium in equities, that basis is where it would show. It does not.

**And the trap that must be named in the essay.** The Reg-T margin-requirement literature already ran the natural experiment and returned null; the literature's own explanation for the null is *substitution into unregulated leverage channels*. That is the same move as C-015's "migration into CCP netting" and Singh's "digital curtain." **A theory whose central quantity is unmeasurable in principle and which explains every null by measurement migration is unfalsifiable.** The eSLR test is worth running precisely because it does not depend on measuring the quantity — it measures the price response to a capacity shock.

## 5. THE FOUR GENUINELY PUBLISHABLE FINDINGS

### FINDING 1 — Leverage against equities has *fallen* relative to equity values throughout the boom
**Claim.** Every publicly identifiable dollar of US credit extended against securities, expressed as a share of the equity value it supposedly levitates, is near the bottom of its own range and has been falling for a decade.
**Number.** FINRA margin debt + H.8 "all loans not elsewhere classified" (the line the Fed's footnote defines as including *"loans for purchasing or carrying securities, including margin loans"*) = **3.495% of Z.1 nonfinancial corporate equity market value at 2026Q1**, against 4.544% (2019Q2) and 5.241% (2015Q4); 45-quarter mean 4.248%; rank 36/45. Margin debt alone: 1.756%, rank 57 of 117 quarters since 1997, median 1.728%. [re-derived, FINRA + FRED]
**Objection.** This measures only Reg-T margin and a US commercial-bank line. It misses prime-brokerage financing inside non-centrally-cleared bilateral repo, securities-based lending booked offshore or at non-bank affiliates, TRS-embedded leverage, and options.
**Answer.** Correct, and it must be stated. But it inverts the burden of proof: every *measurable* leverage channel has shrunk relative to prices, on two independent denominators and three windows. The thesis now survives only in the unmeasured residual — which is a claim about data availability, not about the world.

### FINDING 2 — The NDFI series does not mean what the market thinks, and roughly 28% of its rise is definitional
**Claim.** The H.8 "loans to nondepository financial institutions" line — the headline evidence for a bank-credit-to-non-banks boom — explicitly *excludes* securities purchase-and-carry lending, and more than a quarter of its seven-year growth is a reporting reclassification the Fed never applied to history.
**Numbers.** [all re-derived]
- Fed footnote, line 26: *"Includes loans to mortgage credit intermediaries, business credit intermediaries, and consumer credit intermediaries; loans to private equity funds, insurance companies… hedge funds, pension funds, and securitization vehicles."* Footnote, line 27: *"Includes loans for purchasing or carrying securities, including margin loans…"*
- Documented reclassification **into** NDFI, weeks ending 1-Jan-2025 to 1-Apr-2026: domestically chartered banks $237.7bn + foreign-related institutions $175.2bn = **$412.9bn**, i.e. **28.3%** of the $1,461.4bn rise from $548.3bn (Jul-2019) to $2,009.7bn (Jul-2026). No historical adjustment.
- The single week: NDFI $1,179.9bn (w/e 25-Dec-2024) → **$1,424.3bn** (w/e 1-Jan-2025), **+$244.4bn**, against $113.5bn + $131.6bn = $245.1bn of documented reclassification. It reconciles to **$0.7bn**. The largest one-week move in the series' history is essentially 100% definitional.
- Ex-reclassification, NDFI is **8.10%** of US bank credit in Jul-2026, not the reported 10.19% (vs 4.05% in Jul-2019).
- Meanwhile line 27 — the line that *does* contain securities lending — is **6.48%** of bank credit (Jul-2026) against **6.52%** (Jul-2019). Flat, and flattered, since the same instruction change directed non-purpose securities-based margin loans *into* it.
**Objection.** The reclassification moved existing balances between lines; the underlying lending may still have grown.
**Answer.** It did — ~$1.05trn ex-reclassification over seven years, a doubling of share. The phenomenon is real. What is not real is (i) the headline magnitude and (ii) its interpretation as *equity* financing. It is private credit, mortgage warehousing and securitisation warehousing — credit funding credit — which is a different essay.

### FINDING 3 — The profits-versus-multiple decomposition of 2021–2026 is not identified, and everyone publishing a number for it is publishing an artefact
**Claim.** The widely used decomposition of the recent equity rise into "earnings growth" and "multiple expansion" flips sign across defensible choices of base date, profit-smoothing and sector basis. No point estimate is publishable.
**Number.** Using Z.1 nonfinancial corporate equity as numerator, the multiple's share of the log price move ranges from **−19.4% to +68.2%** [re-derived]:

| window | 4q-avg after-tax profits | 8q-avg | nonfin corp GVA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019Q4→2026Q1 | −14.9% | −12.3% | +43.8% |
| 2021Q4→2025Q4 | +54.7% | −19.4% | +8.7% |
| 2021Q4→2026Q1 | +30.5% | −41.0% | −10.2% |
| 2022Q4→2026Q1 | +68.2% | +67.4% | +67.1% |

Two specific errors sit inside this. Lens 6's "zero multiple expansion (−1.3%)" divided nonfinancial corporate equity by **CPATAX**, which differs from the matched nonfinancial series in **two** ways, not one: it adds financial-sector and rest-of-world profits *and* it applies the IVA and CCAdj (FRED's own title: *"Corporate Profits After Tax **with** Inventory Valuation Adjustment and Capital Consumption Adjustment"*). The project's adversarial reviewer caught the sector mismatch but asserted both series were "without IVA and CCAdj" — that is wrong, and the resulting "+12.12% / 40.7% residual" is itself a spot-quarter figure sitting on a series that moved −12.5% and +9.4% in two of the last five quarters.
**Objection.** "Then you have no finding, only a shrug."
**Answer.** No — the finding is the *robust* part. On all three denominators the valuation level is **flat to up** (−2.5% to +7.9%; Tobin's q +6.9%) across a **+339bp** rise in the 10-year real yield (−1.04% on 31-Dec-2021 → +2.35% on 19-Aug-2026). A discount-rate account predicts a sharp fall on every basis. That is a level fact, not a decomposition, and it needs no contested split to state.

### FINDING 4 — The circuit does not seal, and the two headline pieces of evidence that it does are wrong
**Claim.** The "sealed circuit" premise fails on federal tax data, and the saving-rate statistic used to support the leak story is both misstated and has a counter-example running the wrong way.
**Numbers.** CBO: realised capital gains **8.7% of GDP in 2021**, highest in over 40 years, projected toward 3.7% long-run — implying 12–27% of net holding gains converted to cash annually in taxable accounts alone [secondary; cbo.gov 403s to automated fetch]. And the personal saving rate: 2.7% in June 2026 is **not** the third-lowest of 810 months since 1959 — **28 months are strictly lower and 4 are equal**, ranking it 29th (joint to 32nd). Lower readings occur in 2005 (11), 2006 (5), 2007 (7), **2008 (2)** and **2022 (3)** — including **June 2022 at 2.2%**, during a goods-inflation spike with equities down roughly 19%. [re-derived, FRED PSAVERT]
**Objection.** The 2022 episode is confounded by post-pandemic drawdown.
**Answer.** Yes — which is the point. A statistic that cannot distinguish a wealth-effect leak from a pandemic drawdown cannot be evidence for a wealth-effect leak. The correct claim is the CBO one, and it says the circuit is porous by construction.

## 6. WHAT MUST NOT BE CLAIMED

A competent critic wins on each of these. Cut or repair every one.

**Withdraw entirely**
1. **"Zero multiple expansion / prices tracked profits."** Two-way basis mismatch plus spot-quarter noise. Dead.
2. **"40.7% multiple / 59.3% profits."** The correction is also unpublishable as a point estimate; my grid spans −19% to +68%. Publish the range or publish the level fact.
3. **"Third-lowest saving rate since 1959; only 2005–07 lower."** False. 29th; 2008 and 2022 both appear.
4. **"40.9% of marginal bank credit went to the NDFI-containing line."** Built as a residual when the direct series exists; the direct figure is **28.6%** ($330.4bn of $1,154.1bn), and even that is ~8.5% reclassification. Also mixes SA `LOANINV` with NSA NDFI.
5. **"The 2019Q1–2026Q2 H.8 comparison spans no known break."** Falsified — $412.9bn of documented reclassification, unrevised.
6. **"NDFI lending is the modern financial circuit for equities."** The Fed's footnote excludes securities purchase-and-carry from that line.
7. **Margin-debt (~33%) and buyback (~27%) contributions summed to ~60%.** Both are components of the same aggregate net equity flow that Gabaix–Koijen estimate their single elasticity on. Double-counting. Add the NDFI channel and it exceeds 100%.
8. **"Inverse velocities across the QTC literature, mean 0.585."** Seven rows, six papers, three countries, **one author** (Werner on every one). Not a literature; not triangulation. The 0.432–0.709 spread is the stability diagnostic failing, not a consensus.
9. **Clavero (2017) as "additional empirical support."** His test is an accounting identity (C_R ⊂ C ⟹ C_R/nGDP ≤ C/nGDP) that cannot fail. Do not cite it.

**Repair — the caveat must travel with the claim, or the claim goes**
10. **"Collateral velocity is X."** No independently verifiable ratio exists after 2017, including Singh's own. Say "Singh estimates," never "velocity is."
11. **"Flat on every measurement system with a defensible denominator."** Three incommensurable objects. The ECB's chain length (2.95–3.05) is *higher* than Singh's 2.0 and its authors say chains have **rebounded to pre-Lehman levels** — which contradicts "flat."
12. **"$6,906.97bn."** False precision: Bank of America discloses only to 1dp in trillions (±$50bn). Round to $6.9trn.
13. **Tobin's q "highest of 304 quarters" as evidence of overvaluation.** The Z.1 denominator does not capitalise intangibles, so measured q drifts up mechanically. The project's own facts — 72% of internal funds being depreciation, 90% of gross capex growth absorbed by faster depreciation — are the signature of exactly that shift. The arithmetic is right; the interpretation is not survivable, and the "Minsky puzzle" it sets up is largely mismeasurement.
14. **Damodaran's "required return rose 266bp."** His terminal growth is hard-coded to the risk-free rate, so a rate rise mechanically lifts the implied discount rate. Near-circular.
15. **Bezemer, Ryan-Collins, van Lerven & Zhang used to characterise 2026.** Sample ends **2005**; the shift they document is toward **mortgages and household credit**. The operator's phenomenon is equities (83.7% of holding gains). It does not transfer. Same for JST "Great Mortgaging" and the Favara–Imbs 0.12 elasticity.
16. **"Werner's own falsification test fails on US data."** Weaker than it sounds: Clavero, the literature's own reviewer, says mortgage lending *should* be in the real circuit, so the "Werner-faithful proxy excluding real estate" is not Werner-faithful. You cannot cleanly falsify a test whose proxy nobody knows how to construct. State it as "the proxy is not constructible," which is the real finding.
17. **The premise.** "Goods prices did not inflate" is false for 2021–2026. Date the window explicitly.

**Above all: publish the measurement, not the mechanism.** The corpus has refuted one circuit mechanism (collateral reuse as money creation) with a direct test, has never tested the other (bank credit to NBFIs as asset demand) — and now has strong new evidence that the second one, in its equity form, is shrinking relative to prices. A causal financial-circuit claim published under the operator's own name would not survive a single competent reader with a FRED account.
