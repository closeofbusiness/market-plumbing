# Sources: gold against the US 10-year real yield

Retrieved 18 October 2026 with the script's `fetch` command, one request at a time, at least 5 seconds apart, GET only, no redirect followed, with the neutral User-Agent `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ThirdDerivativeResearch/1.0; non-commercial research)` (no name, no address). The 8 requests and their outcomes are in `fetch_log.csv`; the file versions, with their SHA-256, are in `vintages.csv`. None of the raw files is in this repository. They sit in `~/gold_ry_cache/vintages/`, so a later reader can check a number against the very bytes it came from, because the World Bank and Federal Reserve files are overwritten at their addresses.

## Terms

- **World Bank.** The terms for reusing the Pink Sheet's gold row are unclear. The remainder terms of the Bank's dataset terms apply, and the workbook's own `Description` sheet names, among the sources of its gold series, Bloomberg Finance L.P., Kitco.com, the International Monetary Fund's International Financial Statistics, the London Bullion Market, Platts Metals Week and Thomson Reuters Datastream. The position taken is therefore: **derived statistics only**. Coefficients, standard errors, intervals, test statistics and break dates are published with the World Bank cited (ruled by the supervisor on 18 October 2026: they are not a copy of the data); the gold price, its logarithm, its changes, fitted values and per-month residuals are not. The one per-month file that depends on gold, `rolling36.csv`, is rounded to two decimals. This restriction follows the Bank's redistribution terms; it is not about secrecy, because public GLD data already approximates the series.
- **US Treasury.** Daily Treasury par real yield curve rates are a US government work. The monthly averages are committed with Treasury cited; the raw daily files are not, because the repository does not need them.
- **Federal Reserve Board.** The Board's website puts information it publishes in the public domain, with citation. That position was relayed by a reviewer and was not re-read by the builder. The monthly index values are committed with the Board cited; the raw packages are not.

Cited in the outputs as: World Bank, *Commodity Price Data (The Pink Sheet)*, monthly, updated 2 October 2026. U.S. Department of the Treasury, *Daily Treasury Par Real Yield Curve Rates*, and *Yield Curve Methodology Change Information Sheet*. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, *H.10 Foreign Exchange Rates, Nominal Broad U.S. Dollar Index* (Data Download Program).

## Methods

Each reference below was checked on 18 October 2026 against Crossref's record for its DOI (one request each, neutral User-Agent), except where marked **not re-checked**. Crossref does not always carry the last page, so a page range is marked where only the first page was confirmed.

- Newey, W. K. and West, K. D. (1987), "A simple, positive semi-definite, heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix", *Econometrica* 55(3): 703-708 (title, volume, issue and first page 703 confirmed, DOI 10.2307/1913610; last page 708 **not re-checked**). The covariance, with the Bartlett kernel.
- Kiefer, N. M. and Vogelsang, T. J. (2005), "A new asymptotic theory for heteroskedasticity-autocorrelation robust tests", *Econometric Theory* 21(6) (journal, title, volume, issue and year 2005 confirmed, DOI 10.1017/S0266466605050565; page range 1130-1164 **not re-checked**). The fixed-b critical value, from the cubic in `b = lag / n` as the specification gives it. The coefficients were recalled by the specification's author and not re-read; the build checks the cubic against a seeded simulation (selftest guard G4).
- Andrews, D. W. K. (2003), "Tests for parameter instability and structural change with unknown change point: a corrigendum", *Econometrica* 71(1): 395-397, Table I (**not re-checked**: neither the citation nor the table was checked against the journal's version; the values were read from a third-party copy). The sup-Wald asymptotic critical values printed beside the bootstrap p-value; read by the specification's author from a third-party copy and pinned by the selftest (G6). Never used for a label.
- Politis, D. N. and Romano, J. P. (1994), "The stationary bootstrap", *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 89(428): 1303-1313 (confirmed, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870). The B1 p-value.
- Bai, J. (1997), "Estimation of a change point in multiple regression models", *Review of Economics and Statistics* 79(4): 551-563 (confirmed, DOI 10.1162/003465397557132). The break-date estimator (argmin of the sum of squared residuals) and its 95% interval (anchor 11.03).
- MacKinnon, J. G. (2010), "Critical values for cointegration tests", Queen's University Economics Department Working Paper 1227, Tables 2 (tau_c) and 3 (tau_ct), N = 3, 5% (**not re-checked** by the builder: the Queen's server failed certificate validation on the one request made, and no workaround was tried). The coefficients were read from the paper by the specification's author on 18 October 2026.
- Engle, R. F. and Granger, C. W. J. (1987), "Co-integration and error correction: representation, estimation, and testing", *Econometrica* 55(2): 251-276 (title, volume, issue and first page 251 confirmed, DOI 10.2307/1913236; last page 276 **not re-checked**).
