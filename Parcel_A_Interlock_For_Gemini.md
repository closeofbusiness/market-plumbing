## THE PROBLEM

A single Treasury security can, within one day: sit in a money market fund; be lent out through
a securities-lending programme; be delivered into a repo; be re-pledged by the dealer who
received it; back a stablecoin; and be posted as derivatives margin. In several published
aggregates it will be counted more than once. In others it will be netted away.

Manmohan Singh's collateral-velocity work treats that repetition as THE measurement — the
numerator is a count of claims along chains, and netting it destroys the object. Zoltan Pozsar's
shadow-money aggregates do the opposite: he explicitly nets holdings between intermediaries, and
says gross measures that fail to net "inflated aggregate measures further."

Same fact, two purposes, opposite handling. I want to know how the rest of the statistical world
resolves this — because I am about to build something and I do not want to invent a convention
that already exists, or adopt one that is known to be broken.
