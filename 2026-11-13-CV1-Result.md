# CV1 — does AI-driven managerial confidence explain where the capex money comes from? (13 Nov 2026)

**Short answer: not on free US data, 2022–2025.** The thesis put to us on 13 Nov says AI lowers managers' perceived
uncertainty, so firms commit idle cash to capex and acquisitions sooner, a demand boom that runs ahead of productivity.
Every leg that free data can reach points the other way. Firms' own stated uncertainty rose. Investment outside the AI
build-out did not gain share of the economy. Listed firms outside the AI complex slowed their capex. Corporate cash piles
grew instead of being spent. Within this project's question, "where is the money coming from", this channel is
recorded as **not mattering**. The money for the build-out is not idle corporate cash released by confidence.

How it was tested: every statement was registered before any outcome was read, attacked by an outside model (Grok),
amended (still blind) and then scored once. Specs, attacks and the scorers are under `_research/2026-11-13-CV1-*`,
`bin/cv1_*.py` and `data/cv1_*`. The labels are deliberately one-sided. A pass would only have meant "consistent";
a fail is informative, because the thesis requires each of these things to have happened.

| leg | measure (free source) | result |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 2: perceived uncertainty fell | Atlanta Fed Survey of Business Uncertainty, firms' subjective SD of 12-month sales growth, Dec 2016–Dec 2019 vs 2023–2025, net of policy uncertainty, VIX and forecasters' inflation disagreement | **Rose**: means 3.03 → 3.98 (37 and 36 months); +3.2 baseline residual SDs. UK Decision Maker Panel, described only: ratio 1.04 |
| Stage 4: non-AI investment rose | BEA, nonresidential investment minus IT equipment, software, factories, power and mining structures, % of GDP | **Flat**: 8.08% (2022Q4) → 8.00% (2025Q4), against a slight rise in 2015–19 |
| Stage 4: ex-AI firms accelerated | SEC XBRL capex, 788 common US firms outside the AI complex | **Slowed**: the median firm's growth is 7.3 pp/yr below its 2016–19 pace, net of GDP |
| Stage 4: idle liquidity was drawn down | Fed Z.1, nonfinancial corporate liquid assets | **Built up**: $3.57tn → $4.94tn (+38%) against nominal GDP +17.5% |
| Cash-rich firms led; hard-asset sectors led | Same firm panel, terciles of cash/assets; manufacturing and mining vs trade and services | **Neither**: both gaps negative |

Caveats:
- **Survey uncertainty is nominal.** The rise survives a control for inflation-forecast disagreement, but this is still an
  aggregate panel, not a firm-level AI comparison.
- **The firm panel is incomplete.** It covers about 41% of ex-AI listed capex. Capex is worldwide consolidated, and SIC
  codes are current, not historical.
- **The thesis's two survey citations needed correcting:**
  - Decision-making is the *third* motive for AI spending, not the top one (NBER WP 34984).
  - The "89% report no productivity impact" figure is real (WP 34836), but it covers all firms and says nothing about
    capital deployment.
- **The sector AI-use comparison (P6) was not built.** Census refused the download (403).
- **The ex-post write-down test (P7)** is first scorable in April 2029.

This is consistent with earlier published work: San Francisco Fed Economic Letter 2026-13 finds no capex growth outside
firms whose earnings calls are AI-positive.
