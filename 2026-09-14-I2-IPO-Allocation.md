# I2 — who was allocated the 2026 IPOs (14 September 2026)

**Status: ANSWERED, and the answer is mostly "nobody can tell."** Ranked item 5. Data
`data/i2_ipo_allocation/` (6 CSVs). Supervisor-verified 14 Sep.

**The denominator, and it must travel with every number:** 2026 operating-company primary proceeds are
**$111.94bn including SpaceX's $75.00bn**, or **$36.94bn excluding it.** SpaceX raised two-thirds of the
year's money on its own.

## A correction to my own brief

I briefed that SC 13G/13D filings were *"the closest free thing to an allocation."* **They are not, and the
agent was right to say so.** Of 45 filings across 8 issuers, the named >5% holders are overwhelmingly
**pre-IPO founders, venture backers, PE sponsors and strategic partners** — Musk, Google, Founders Fund,
Honeywell, Advent, Carlyle, Hellman & Friedman, Benchmark, Tiger Global, Foundation Capital, Devon Energy.
I checked the list myself and it holds.

These filings exist **because going public creates a reporting obligation for stakes that already existed.**
They are a cap-table snapshot, not an allocation record. And the deeper problem cannot be fixed by finding a
"purer" holder: **a genuinely new holder may have bought on day two in the secondary market, which raises
the company nothing.** Only the priced allocation counts toward the $112bn.

## The answer

**Only prospectus cornerstones speak to allocation at pricing**, and they are a small share:

| | with SpaceX in the denominator | without SpaceX |
|---|---:|---:|
| cornerstone $ as share of 2026 primary proceeds | **6.6%** | **19.9%** |
| including Medline (priced Dec 2025) | 8.4% | 23.0% |

**So 77–93% of the proceeds went to buyers the underwriters never named publicly** — ordinary confidential
book-building. **The three-fold swing between the two columns *is* SpaceX:** it raised two-thirds of the
money and named zero cornerstones, so its presence in the denominator changes the answer by a factor of three.

## What the filings did reveal

- **Sovereign wealth is visible where it files.** Medline's post-IPO holders include **GIC Private Limited
  at 14.64%** and the **Abu Dhabi Investment Authority at 5.9%.**
- **A recurring crossover set.** Bending Spoons disclosed no cornerstones, yet **Baillie Gifford (15.23%)**
  and **Durable Capital Partners (7.3%, event date exactly the IPO date)** appear as named holders — and both
  are disclosed cornerstones on *other* 2026 deals. The same handful of funds recurs across the class even
  where undisclosed. This is the one soft exception to the pre-IPO rule.
- **Sponsors underwrote their own sponsors' deals.** Blackstone Securities Partners and TCG Capital Markets
  (Carlyle) both sat in Medline's syndicate — which is why that prospectus heads the section
  **"Underwriting (Conflicts of Interest)"**. Blackstone underwrote Jersey Mike's too. Medline's syndicate
  ran to **38 banks**.
- **A strategic holding worth noting:** AMD filed a 13F showing a direct 794,467-share position in Cerebras
  — a competitor holding a stake, not an asset manager.

## The pattern this makes, and it is the third instance

**The buyers of the IPO boom are unnamed by construction — exactly like the buyers of equity generally.**
That is now three findings of the same shape in two days:

1. **The household residual** — 94.6% of net new equity absorbed by a sector computed as a leftover.
2. **AI financing vehicles** — four of six firms name no entity; one explicitly denies having any.
3. **IPO allocation** — 77–93% of proceeds to buyers nobody discloses.

In each case the information is not missing by accident. **Book-building is confidential by design, the Fed
residual is a residual by construction, and private financing is private.** The limit is structural, not a
gap in our searching — and that is worth saying in the answer rather than carrying as three open items.

## Method notes worth keeping

- **13F info-table "value" is in whole dollars in the modern XML**, not the legacy thousands. Proved by two
  independent filers on SpaceX implying $170.90 and $170.86 per share — a near-exact match that only works
  on the dollars reading.
- **13G and 13F need not agree.** Coatue's Cerebras filings reconcile exactly at 7,011,028 shares; Atreides'
  do not (13G 10,543,836 vs 13F 3,810,086). Beneficial ownership and investment discretion are different
  legal tests. Reported as observed, not reconciled.
- Every syndicate's share counts summed to the filing's own stated total — which is how a transcription
  error on Jersey Mike's was caught and fixed.
