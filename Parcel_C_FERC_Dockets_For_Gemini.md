# Parcel C — the FERC large-load dockets · for Gemini Flash 3.7 (high)

*22 August 2026. Third of three single-purpose parcels. **This is the overdue calendar item** —
due 17 August, five days late, and the one that motivated building the calendar in the first
place.*

**What this parcel is for.** Three pre-registered persona-panel positions resolve on these
filings, by three different methods that agree on nothing else. The panel called it its
strongest non-prior signal. It is a pure document-retrieval task — FERC eLibrary is public — and
it is the single best fit for a search model in the whole project.

**Note on scope.** This serves **A2**, the persona capability, not A12. Whether A2 stays live is
still an open decision. This parcel is written so the answer is worth having either way: if the
personas are parked, the filings still tell us whether a transferable flexible-load service class
now exists, which is a real fact about how compute demand gets financed.

**Design note (C-045).** Everything below asks what the filings *say*. There is no number here
for a model to agree with, which is why this parcel is the most reliable of the three.

## THE PROCEEDING

On **18 June 2026** the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission issued **Section 206 show-cause
orders to all FERC-jurisdictional RTOs and ISOs**, concerning tariff treatment of **large loads
above 50 MW at above 69 kV**, and of **co-location**. The orders carried a **60-day clock**, so
compliance responses were due **on or around 17 August 2026** — five days ago.

Related background, which I believe to be correct but have not verified myself: on **29 January
2026, effective 30 January**, FERC accepted PJM's Replacement Generation Interconnection Process,
allowing Capacity Interconnection Rights to transfer from a retiring facility to a replacement
resource at the same site, same substation and same voltage.

## FIRST, ESTABLISH THE BASICS — I MAY HAVE THESE WRONG

1. **Confirm or correct the 18 June 2026 orders**: the date, the docket numbers, the exact
   scope (the 50 MW / 69 kV thresholds), and the response deadline.
2. **Name the RTOs and ISOs that received them.** I have been carrying "six" without having
   checked it. Give me the actual list, and note whether ERCOT is included — my understanding
   is that it is not FERC-jurisdictional for this purpose, but confirm rather than assume.
3. **Confirm the filings were actually made**, and on what dates. If any RTO sought or received
   an extension, that matters as much as the content and I need to know.

## THEN, THE TWO THINGS I NEED FROM THE FILINGS

These are the questions three pre-registered positions turn on. Answer each **per RTO**, not in
aggregate — the whole point is whether the outcome is uniform or venue-by-venue.

**QUESTION 1 — Does a TRANSFERABLE flexible-load service class appear?**

Not merely a flexible, curtailable or non-firm service class — a **transferable** one. The
distinction is the entire question. A curtailable-load tariff that a customer may take but not
assign, sell or trade is a service; a right that can be conveyed to another party is an
instrument. Report, per RTO:
   - whether any new large-load or flexible-load service class is proposed at all;
   - whether the proposed right is **assignable or transferable**, and under what conditions;
   - whether any secondary market, auction or bilateral transfer mechanism is contemplated;
   - the exact tariff language on transferability, quoted briefly.

**QUESTION 2 — Do the RTOs DEFEND firm-service-only tariffs?**

The opposite outcome: RTOs responding that existing firm-service-only arrangements are just and
reasonable, and proposing no flexible-load class. Report, per RTO:
   - whether the filing defends the status quo or proposes reform;
   - the grounds given — reliability, cost allocation, discrimination, administrability;
   - whether the RTO's own market monitor, or state commissions, filed contrary comments.

## THEN, THREE SUPPORTING QUESTIONS

4. **Co-location specifically.** The orders cover co-located load. What do the filings propose
   for generation co-located with large load behind or adjacent to the meter — the arrangement
   at issue in the earlier Susquehanna/Talen matter? Is co-located load treated as network load,
   as something else, or left unresolved?

5. **How wide is the PJM Capacity Interconnection Right precedent?** Do any of the other RTOs
   propose anything analogous — a transferable interconnection or capacity right? Or does CIR
   transferability remain a PJM-only construct? I am told ERCOT prices queue discipline through
   a security deposit of roughly $100k/MW instead of anything transferable; confirm or correct.

6. **What happens next, and when.** Comment deadlines, reply deadlines, and any date by which
   FERC must act. If this proceeding has a further dated step, I want it — I am maintaining a
   calendar of resolution dates and this one has already slipped once.

## HOW TO ANSWER

Per RTO, per question, keep it tight:
- **Verdict:** TRANSFERABLE CLASS PROPOSED / FLEXIBLE BUT NOT TRANSFERABLE / STATUS QUO DEFENDED
  / NOT ADDRESSED / FILING NOT FOUND
- **Source:** docket number, accession number, filing date, page or section. A link to eLibrary.
- **The language itself:** a short quote where the tariff wording carries the meaning. Precision
  matters more than fluency here — "may be assigned with Transmission Provider consent" and
  "shall not be assignable" are the whole answer.
- **Where you cannot find a filing, say so explicitly** and say where you looked. A missing
  filing is a finding.

Do not summarise the regulatory background back to me beyond correcting what I got wrong above.
Do not tell me what it means for the power sector. I need the documents read accurately; the
interpretation is mine.
