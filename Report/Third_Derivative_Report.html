The Third Derivative

Where value actually accrues when a capacity overbuild leaves something behind.

2 August 2026  ·  58 agents, 3 adversarial verifier rounds  ·  Step-ladder protocol
01Executive summary

The pattern is real, already named, and roughly ten times rarer than the framing assumes

The concept holds — but only as a narrow special case, not as a theory of technological succession. Three of its original claims had to be retracted, its central ordinal had to be rebuilt on a different object, and about a third of the assembled evidence contradicts it. What survives is sharper and more useful than what went in: a nine-condition test whose main value is telling you when the framework does not apply.

The four things worth carrying away

  • It has a name. Carlota Perez's installation → frenzy → turning point → deployment, with William Janeway's "productive bubbles" as the sharper statement of the mechanism. The electricity illustration is Paul David's 1990 argument almost exactly.
  • Counting technology generations cannot work. Every third order in the corpus is some other case's first order. The fix is to stop counting technologies and count claims on one metered capacity.
  • Value does not go to the asset buyer. Cogent and Level 3 bought the fibre and earn utility returns; Netflix and Cloudflare bought metered services priced off written-down assets and took the equity value.
  • Applied to AI today, accelerators fail the test. Power, interconnection and shells pass — but predict incumbent capture, not a new cohort.
02The pattern

A primary innovation pays out only after something that looks like waste while it happens

The shape is consistent across the cases that survive scrutiny: an innovation creates capacity; a reorganisation or overbuild strands that capacity and destroys the capital behind it; a later operator runs the same physical asset on a cost basis the builder could never have underwritten.

On the two illustrations that started this work. Electricity → shop floor → mass production, and internet → dot-com/fibre bubbles → cloud. Both were offered to identify the pattern rather than to defend it, and both contain inaccuracies. They are kept as the framing device; the evidence sits on the case library. The corrections are in §7 of the concept map — most consequentially, AWS and Azure come off the list, and Cloudflare is the only well-chosen name on it.

03Prior art

The concept is a re-description of four literatures that have already fought these battles

Seventy-four candidate names were gathered and then cut hard: a third were duplicates (Perez alone occupied eleven slots for what is one book), one was a fabricated attribution returning zero search results, and one was included purely because its title matched while meaning something else entirely.

On the name. "Third derivative" carries a false mathematical promise — a real third derivative is computed with respect to a specified variable, and nothing here is. Retained as the house label because it communicates, but it should never be presented as though the ordinal were measured.

04Retractions

Three claims in the original framing do not survive the evidence

The bubble's causal role downgrades from creating the discount to creating the asset in irrational quantity, and supplying the cessation event that dislodges it. That is a smaller claim, and a defensible one.

05The repair

Stop counting technologies; count claims on one metered capacity

The ordinal becomes meaningful when it is anchored on something enumerable. Register four things in writing, timestamped, before any layer assignment:

This supplies the stopping rule the concept lacked. You can run the frame again on a different metered input, but not on the same one — once capacity is held at written-down basis by an operator whose business works, there is no further write-down to harvest. The number of write-downs on a given asset is finite and typically one. That comes from the accounting, not from a judgement about when generativity decays.

It also costs the electricity illustration outright: there is no single metered input whose price collapse the assembly line consumed. That a repair excludes the flagship example is evidence it is doing work rather than accommodating.

06The test

Nine conditions, of which four are necessary — and the exclusions matter more than the passes

A second-order overbuild produces a third order if and only if the written-down capacity is legally dislodgeable, is a general-purpose input to a different industry than the builders competed in, and faces surviving physical output demand when the revenue model dies.

The exclusion rule is the most valuable output

If the candidate overbuild is competing output capacity in the market it was built to serve — more solar modules, more airline seats, more merchant megawatts, more of the same tokens sold to the same buyers — the framework does not apply. In that region Klepper and the capital cycle are correct: the cull is endogenous, needs no bubble at all, and the winners are survivors with a lineage. Forecast consolidation, not succession.

The framework's entire domain is the complement: overbuilds whose residue is an input to somebody else's industry. That is the only reason "different people" appears at all — not because busts create new cohorts, but because the incumbents are, by construction, in the wrong industry to be the buyers.

07Evidence

A third of the documented cases contradict the framework, and those were the informative ones

What the contradicting cases establish

  • The bubble is not necessary. China's hyperscale cloud (Alibaba, Tencent) was built organically from cash flow with no preceding fibre bust. India Stack, M-PESA and Brazil's Pix are public infrastructure with live third orders and no malinvestment phase anywhere in the chain.
  • Cleantech 1.0 is the cleanest disconfirmation. A real bubble, real capital destroyed — and the residue did not seed the 2015–25 battery and EV generation, which came from Tesla (founded 2003, before the bubble) and Asian cell manufacturers who never had a bust. Solyndra's building transferred to Seagate for $90.2m and became data storage; the chemistry-specific tooling was worthless.
  • Overbuild alone is profoundly insufficient. Soviet industry, Japan post-1990, European 3G and US nuclear all built vast capacity and produced nothing. The deciding variable is not scale of investment — it is whether control can move.
  • The Black Death is the natural experiment. Identical shock, identical labour scarcity, opposite outcomes east and west of the Elbe — wage labour versus the second serfdom. Institutions, not the shock, determine the third order.
08Value capture

The value does not go to whoever buys the asset

This is the finding with the most direct commercial bite, and it surprised both sides of the argument it was meant to settle.

The prospective form. After inference prices collapse, value goes to holders of scarce complements — proprietary distribution, regulated data, workflow lock-in, firm power and interconnection rights. Not to whoever buys distressed accelerators, and not to a new cohort of model labs.

09Live application

AI accelerators fail the test; the grid passes but predicts incumbent capture

Six current capacity events were scored against the nine conditions with verified anchor prices, dated falsification windows and named failure conditions, and registered before any outcome is known. The full register is in the project folder.

12Limits

It can exclude; it cannot select — and anyone using it to pick winners is misusing it

The defence that died. "Nobody forecasts the third order" is not available. Gilder's Telecosm, HP's 1998 utility-computing division, Sun Grid at $1/CPU-hour in 2006 and Hastings naming Netflix in 1997 were all ex ante and right in kind. They were destroyed anyway — which is good for the mechanism, because it shows foresight is not the scarce input, and fatal to any claim that this framework confers unique foresight.
