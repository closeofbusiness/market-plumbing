# State of the argument

Last updated: 2026-08-21

## Original framing

Working title: *The Collateral Multiplier and Systemic Contagion of the AI, Private
Equity, and Private Credit Nexus.* Framed on Pozsar / Manmohan Singh balance-sheet
method and NBFI structural credit analysis. Four pillars:

- **A — ETF plumbing.** Securities lending cash reinvestment, intraday collateral
  velocity, AP balance sheet capacity.
- **B — AI infrastructure debt.** Compute ABF, DDTLs, GPU SPVs, neocloud structures,
  hyperscaler off-balance-sheet borrowing.
- **C — PE / private credit / life insurer flywheel.** Sponsor-owned insurers, FABNs,
  NAV loans, CFOs, SRT wraps.
- **D — Equity valuation collateralisation.** Prime brokerage borrowing base, TRS,
  corporate debt capacity.

Background reading: Andromeda Capital Management, *The Silver Bullet | AI Credit:
Heads I Win, Tails You Lose*, August 2026 (Gallo, Aney, Pan, Koor).

## What holds up

- The instrument-level analogy is BIS's own: an FX swap resembles a repo with a
  currency rather than a security as collateral, with the payment obligation booked
  off balance sheet. Same for collateral reuse in repo.
- Pillar C circularity is well documented. Athene: $350bn statutory assets against
  $4.6bn surplus, a 1.3% surplus-to-liability ratio versus a national average above
  7%; over $50bn in affiliated investments; roughly half the portfolio in private
  assets. Insurers act as both borrowers against and guarantors of the same private
  credit exposure.
- Distress data is real: software loan distress ratio above 25%, PIK approaching 10%
  of private credit interest income, public BDC redemptions breaching the 5% gate.
- CoreWeave's July 2026 DDTL repricing — marketed at SOFR+425 / issue price 99,
  cleared at SOFR+550 / OID 97 — is a clean observable of creditor repricing.

## What had to be corrected

**1. Collateral velocity is not the growth engine.** Singh's own position is that
velocity has been stuck for two to three years because Basel III leaves no exemption
for reserves or Treasuries — the dealer balance sheet cannot pull more trucks. ECB
SFTDS evidence: ~11.6% of European repo volume relies on reused securities, roughly
€49bn/day, and the paper finds *against* the liquidity-windfall hypothesis.

**2. FX swaps show the same plateau.** 2025 Triennial: FX swap turnover $4tn/day, up
only 5% from April 2022; share of FX turnover fell from 51% to 42%. Growth was in
spot (+42%), forwards (+60%) and options (+108%). BIS attributes April 2025 to
tariff-driven hedging by investors raising hedge ratios from historically low levels,
not to leverage-seeking. EUR/USD FX swap volume actually fell 14%.

**3. The stock story is separate from the turnover story and does hold.** Outstanding
dollar obligations in FX swaps/forwards/currency swaps exceeded $80tn (end-June 2022),
larger than dollar T-bills, repo and CP combined. Non-banks outside the US: ~$25–26tn
of "missing" debt versus ~$13tn on balance sheet, up from $17tn in 2016. Non-US banks:
$35–39tn. Combined outstanding across the three instruments was $94tn at end-2021 —
above global cross-border portfolio investment ($81tn) and more than triple global
trade ($28.5tn). That ratio is the strongest available evidence that the driver is
portfolio hedging, not real-economy need.

**4. Pillar C cannot be quantified as money creation.** No published series links
FABNs, NAV loans, CFOs or SRT wraps into a shadow-money aggregate. FSB itself flags
severe limitations in private credit data across statistical and regulatory reporting.

## Current synthesis: where the money comes from

The "where is all this money coming from" question decomposes into three unrelated
mechanisms. Conflating them is what makes the boom look inexplicable.

**Equity valuations — a price effect, not a money effect.** Gabaix & Koijen's
inelastic markets result: a dollar of net flow into equities moves aggregate market
capitalisation by roughly five dollars. Buybacks shrink free float, index
concentration funnels flows to the same names, and passive vehicles are price
insensitive by construction. A few hundred billion of flow produces trillions of
capitalisation. The multiplier is symmetric on the way down.

**AI capex — reallocated corporate cash flow plus a thin credit layer.** Hyperscaler
free cash flow is declining as capex absorbs it; money that went to buybacks now goes
to datacentres. That is a change in the use of existing profits. The genuinely new
credit is the marginal layer: $300bn+ raised by hyperscalers and neoclouds, AI-linked
bonds above 10% of the US IG market, plus GPU SPV and private credit financing.
Underneath it, Kalecki: at 6%+ deficits with full employment, the government deficit
is corporate profit. That flow appears in no monetary aggregate.

**Gold — sovereign reserve rotation, and a bet against the above.** Not a liquidity
symptom. Central banks bought 1,000t+ for three years running, 863t in 2025, and a
record-for-any-Q2 289t in Q2 2026 during the steepest quarterly price decline in a
decade. WGC 2026 survey: 89% expect global CB gold holdings to rise; 74% expect the
dollar's share of reserves to fall over five years. Price-insensitive structural
demand.

**The monetary aggregate says no boom.** M2 ~$23.2tn (June 2026), +5.5% y/y — roughly
nominal GDP pace. QT ended in late 2025; the Fed uses reserve management purchases;
the market prices hikes under Warsh. What is expanding is the non-bank layer: NBFI
+9.4% in 2024, double the banking sector, narrow measure +12% to $76.3tn.

**Implication for the report.** The fragility is not a money overhang that must
unwind. It is that a very large valuation stock rests on a small marginal flow, in a
system where the collateral and FX-swap plumbing is already balance-sheet constrained
and cannot absorb a reversal by expanding velocity.

## Corrected factual points to not repeat

- Gold is **not** at a record. Peak $5,586 in January 2026; fell ~16% in Q2;
  ~+11% since early August from around $4,000. Currently well below the January high.
- H1 2026 central bank gold buying (~345t) was the **weakest half since 2022** after
  the Q1 revision. The record Q2 was largely catch-up. Both statements are true.
- Kevin Warsh chairs the Fed; 2026 market pricing is for hikes, not cuts.
