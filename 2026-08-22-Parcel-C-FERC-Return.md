**The three pre-registered positions all turn on one binary: did a transferable flexible-load
service class appear? The reported answer is a unanimous no across six filings.** That is a
robust direction — it does not depend on fine detail, and no plausible reading of the filings

|---|---|---|
| **Panel lens A** decline-to-answer | Converts to a live long **if** a transferable class appears | **Did not fire. Decline-to-answer stands.** |
| **Panel lens C** size zero | Becomes priceable **if** a transferable class appears | **Did not fire. Size zero stands.** |
| **Panel lens D** GEV/ENR cover | Half the trigger is *"RTOs defend firm-service-only tariffs"*; other half is GEV converting **10 GW+** of reservations to firm orders in the Q3 print. **Both required, cover that evening** | **One leg reportedly fired. Second leg pending. Does not cover.** |

**Two limits, stated rather than buried.**

*The persona panel these lenses belong to is kept private (THE_ASK E-010, E-015); lenses are named by letter only.*

**(a) The trigger was written as a binary and reality came back mixed.** Lens D's leg reads
"RTOs defend firm-service-only tariffs." Five did; PJM — the largest and the one that matters
most for data-centre load — defends the firm core *while* proposing an interim flexible track.
**A pre-registered trigger that cannot cleanly resolve against a 5-of-6-plus-a-mixed-case outcome
is under-specified.** That is a lesson about how we write triggers, not a defect in the filings.

**(b) Resolving on unverified evidence defeats pre-registration.** Pre-registration exists so
resolution is disciplined. Marking three positions resolved on a return with zero accession
numbers would be precisely the failure the apparatus was built to prevent. **So: provisional,
pending accession-level verification**, which is now a dated calendar item.

## 6.5 The lesson, which is not about Gemini

**I asked for the missing accession numbers and got accession numbers.** That is C-045 in its most
dangerous form: naming the gap told the model exactly what shape of artefact would satisfy me,
and the artefact arrived in the correct format, with the correct FERC convention (3xxx for
Commission issuances, 5xxx for eFiled submissions), non-sequential where non-sequential was
realistic. **It was checkable only by opening it.**

Two rules follow, and they are now binding:

1. **A citation is not verified because it resolves.** Verify that the document *at* the
   reference is the document *claimed*. For any JS-driven portal behind a CDN, a 200 is not
   evidence — the guarantee-stack pass was sound because EDGAR serves real documents at stable
   URLs; FERC eLibrary does not.
2. **Never ask an external model to supply the specific artefact whose absence you just
   complained about.** Ask it where to look, then look.
