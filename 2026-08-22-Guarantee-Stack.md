# The disclosed contingent-guarantee stack

Written to answer one charge from external review: *are you retreating to the unmeasurable
because the measurable did not cooperate?* Nine filers, all primary SEC sources with accession
numbers, three issuer lenses plus a beneficiary lens.

**Verdict: the charge fails on quantum and holds partly on value — and the mechanism is not the
one we named. The guarantee is the visible minority case; the unrecorded lease is about 4.9x larger like-for-like [C-116: "13x" divided future leases by live guarantees only].**

*22 August 2026.*

# SYNTHESIS: THE DISCLOSED CONTINGENT-GUARANTEE STACK

**Nine filers read, all primary SEC sources, three lenses. Verdict on the charge: it fails, but not in the direction the project expected.**

---

## 1. THE NUMBER, ON ONE BASIS

**Basis:** *Maximum potential amount of future payments under a guarantee of a third party's obligation, as disclosed by the guarantor.* This is ASC 460-10-50-4 and the credit-derivative-seller disclosures in ASC 815-10-50, treated as one measurement concept — a warrant I take from Alphabet, which pairs its ASC 460 and ASC 815 ceilings in a single MD&A sentence under one shared phrase.

**The methodological move that makes the sum possible** (neither lens did this): Meta's headline $46.03bn is ASC 810 and bundles equity carrying value, lease commitments and funding commitments alongside the guarantee. I have **decomposed it and taken only the guarantee component — the ~$28bn RVG threshold** — which is what makes Meta commensurable with the other four. The $46.03bn itself is not summable with anything and must not be used here.

**TOTAL CONTRACTED: $228.3bn.**

**Alternative cut, offered because the effectiveness line is genuinely arguable:** if Meta's $28bn moves to the not-yet-effective tier on the grounds that its leases commence in 2029 (the difference between Meta and NVIDIA here is disclosure *form*, not economics), the split is **$58.2bn live / $170.1bn contracted-not-live**. Total unchanged.

**Excluded, and why:** Microsoft's $13bn and Amazon's $35bn OpenAI commitments and Alphabet's $21.9bn VIE funding commitments (investments, not guarantees of another party's obligation to a lender); Meta's $46.03bn ASC 810 figure and its $12.31bn lease and $2.92bn carrying-value components (double counts); Amazon's standby LCs (wrong purpose); NVIDIA's optional ~3.8GW of additional Portsmouth credit support (**unquantified — do not impute the ~$94bn a pro-rata scaling would give**); all purchase obligations and all ASC 842 lease figures.

**Coverage: 6 of 6 filers determinate. 4 quantify, 2 are clean negatives.** That is unusually complete. But quality is not: only Alphabet gives a re-measured series with comparatives ($0 at 2025-12-31 → $16.94bn at 2025-12-31 → $43.785bn). NVIDIA's $3.5bn is an inception ceiling repeated verbatim across two filings four months apart with no as-of date attached — one disclosure carried forward, not two observations.

### The number the guarantee stack sits behind — about 4.9x bigger like-for-like

> **[C-116, 23 Sep]** This heading said "13x", dividing leases *signed but not yet commenced* (all future) by *live* guarantees
> ($86.2bn). Future against future — against the **$228.3bn total contracted** printed above — it is **4.9x**. The point stands; the ratio did not.

**ASC 842 leases signed but not yet commenced, six filers, one standard:**

Meta $278.99bn + Microsoft $329.1bn + Oracle $260bn + Amazon $137.2bn + Alphabet $85.2bn + NVIDIA $32.4bn = **$1,122.9bn**.

Vintages: four at 2026-06-30, Oracle 2026-05-31, NVIDIA 2026-04-26. Prior-period equivalents sum to **~$417.4bn** across vintages spanning eight months — so roughly **2.7x growth**, an order-of-magnitude statement, not a precise multiple. **Disclosed by 6 of 6. Tagged in XBRL by 0 of 6.** Excluded add-ons: Meta ~$68bn (July 2026), Alphabet $5.8bn short-term and $9.9bn PPA-as-lease.

**This is the finding that reorganises the project.** The credit support is usually *not* a guarantee. At Hut 8's Beacon Point the notes are expressly **not guaranteed by anyone** — Hut 8 says so — and the lease covenant alone earns Baa2. Of the $16.58bn of investment-grade-covenant project debt identified on the beneficiary side, **$8.40bn rests on a bare lease to an unnamed IG tenant and $8.18bn on a guarantee**. The guarantee is the visible minority case. The unrecorded lease is the majority case, is about 4.9x larger in aggregate like-for-like [C-116], and is equally off balance sheet.

## 2. IS THE CHARGE ANSWERED?

**Partly. The ceiling is measurable and measured; the value is measurable only from the beneficiary side, at 9.5% coverage; and the claim that motivated the mechanism is false.**

**(a) The quantum: YES.** $86.2bn live, $228.3bn contracted, 6/6 determinate coverage, every component read in a filing. There is no retreat to the unmeasurable on the size of the guarantee stack. That half of the charge fails outright.

**(b) The value: the coverage ratio is 9.5%.** The two-sided test is the strongest evidence the project has produced and also the source of its sharpest gap. Alphabet discloses $43.785bn of backstop notional and names no counterparty. The beneficiary side independently identifies **$8.183bn** of Google-backstopped project debt at three filers — Hut 8 River Bend $3.25bn, TeraWulf Wulf Compute $3.20bn, Cipher Compute $1.733bn, all with Fluidstack as tenant. **$8.183bn traceable against $86.2bn of disclosed guarantee ceiling = 9.5%. Roughly 90% of the disclosed guarantee ceiling has no beneficiary visible anywhere on EDGAR** — Meta's Venture, NVIDIA's SB Energy and unnamed "early-stage companies", and Oracle's unnamed lessor are not SEC filers. Every traceable dollar of guarantee-enabled project debt has a single guarantor: Google.

**And the price of the guarantee is disclosed — but only by the party paying it.** TeraWulf $515.5m + Cipher $544.5m = **$1.060bn** of penny-warrant fair value capitalised as consideration to Google for the backstop, against $4.933bn of backstopped debt (21.5% of principal). The guarantor reports the same service at approximately nil — Meta records no liability, Alphabet calls financial-guarantee fair value not material — and Alphabet does not break out the warrants it *received*. **The consideration is invisible on both sides of the guarantor's books and fully priced on the beneficiary's.** Two caveats: Hut 8 has the identical Google backstop at River Bend and discloses no consideration at all, so even the beneficiary-side price has 2-of-3 coverage; and TeraWulf's and Cipher's warrants are two instances of one counterparty's standard deal terms, **not two independent price discoveries**.

**(c) The template claim: REFUTED. Do not soften this.** "A Big Tech lease guarantee roughly halves a project's financing cost" is false on the only measure any filer discloses. No issuer states a spread to any benchmark, so the ratio is unverifiable; on coupon, the reduction is never a halving:

| Comparison | Coupons | Δ | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Applied Digital, same sponsor, 110 days: CoreWeave → IG hyperscaler | 9.250% @97 → 6.750% @98 | 250bp | 27% |
| Cipher, same sponsor, 90 days: Google backstop → direct IG | 7.125% → 6.125% | 100bp | 14% |
| **Hut 8, same sponsor, 38 days, both 2042, both amortising, both non-recourse: Google backstop → direct AA- tenant** | **6.192% → 6.129%** | **6.3bp** | **1.0%** |
| Widest observed, cross-sponsor: Galaxy/CoreWeave → Cipher/IG | 9.875% → 6.125% | 375bp | 38% |

Maximum observed reduction: **38%**. A halving requires 50%.

**The Hut 8 pair is the single best-controlled observation in the dataset and neither lens drew it out: a Google backstop of a non-investment-grade tenant prices within 6.3 basis points of a direct AA-or-higher tenant covenant.** River Bend also amortises two years earlier (shorter WAL), which should price it tighter, so 6.3bp is if anything a floor — though no rating is disclosed for River Bend and the comparison is not curve-adjusted. The Cipher pair says 100bp for the same substitution. **The backstop discount is somewhere between 6 and 100bp and the two same-sponsor observations do not agree.**

**Two further corrections carried forward, both filing-supported:** (i) the "~100bp over Nvidia's own 30-year paper" claim is not reproducible — NVIDIA has issued no bond since 2020, its only long bond is 3.70% due 2060, and 6.129% is **243bp** over that coupon; (ii) **NVIDIA is not identified as the Hut 8 tenant in any filing** — Hut 8 says "a high-investment-grade company (i.e., rated AA- or higher)" and the word NVIDIA appears zero times in its 10-Q. Demote to press attribution. The rating band narrows the candidate set; do not name.

**(d) One thing the coupon comparison cannot see, and it favours the mechanism.** Cipher paid $544.5m of warrants for a backstop worth 100bp on $1.733bn — about $87m of interest over the note life. **Six times the coupon saving.** TeraWulf's $515.5m is 16.1% of principal, a ~322bp-per-year equivalent over five years, inside but near the top of the observed band. The warrant is not priced off the bond; it backstops a 10–15 year lease, and no filer discloses the lease contract value against which it would pencil. **The beneficiaries are not buying basis points. They are buying the existence of the financing.** That is created creditworthiness with a price on it, and it is the one place in the project where the mechanism is measured rather than asserted.

**(e) A finding that reframes how much the covenant is worth.** Beacon Point: tenant rated **AA- or higher**, project notes rated **Baa2**. Seven notches of leakage. And Galaxy's CoreWeave-tenant project bond at 9.875% prices **25bp wider than CoreWeave's own unsecured 9.625% 2032**. Together: project structure alone is worth nothing or less than nothing; the tenant covenant is worth everything; and what it buys is a lift *into* investment grade, not *to* the tenant's rating.

## 3. WHAT WOULD CLOSE THE GAP

The requirements largely exist and are being answered with boilerplate. Six named fixes:

1. **XBRL tagging of ASC 842-20-50-8.** $1,122.9bn disclosed by six filers, tagged by none. This is an SEC Inline XBRL matter (Reg S-T Rule 405) plus a taxonomy element, not a FASB change. **This is the mechanical explanation for how the $662bn and $970bn ghosts got into circulation** — with no structured series to pull, intermediaries reconstruct one and the reconstruction acquires a provenance it never had. Highest ratio of fix-cost to value in the whole list.
2. **Extend ASC 842-20-50-8 to require the maturity analysis that 842-20-50-6 already requires for commenced leases.** Amazon alone provides one (68% of its $137.2bn falls after 2030), proving feasibility. Without it, $985bn of the trillion has no duration.
3. **Require disclosure of the fair value of consideration *received* for issuing a guarantee.** ASC 460-10-50-4 requires the carrying amount of the liability; nothing requires the guarantor to disclose what it was paid. This is the exact mirror of what TeraWulf and Cipher already disclose, and adding it would convert a $1.060bn beneficiary-side observation into a guarantor-side series. The single most targeted fix available.
4. **Counterparty disaggregation of guarantee ceilings above a threshold.** Alphabet names none of the $43.785bn; NVIDIA names none of the $3.5bn; four beneficiaries never name an IG tenant — a perfect inverse correlation (non-IG tenants named, IG tenants anonymous). Closeable by SEC staff guidance under the existing ASC 460-10-50-4 "how it arose" requirement and ASC 275 concentration rules, without new standard-setting.
5. **Pull single-tenant data-centre lease-backed project notes into Reg AB.** The multi-tenant colocation securitisations file Form ABS-15G under Rule 15Ga-2 — 89 filings from 34 securitizers, including QTS, Vantage, Switch, STACK, Compass. Hut 8, Cipher, Applied Digital and Galaxy do not. Widening the Item 1101(c) definition of asset-backed security would bring third-party due diligence findings and Rule 17g-7 rating disclosure to the $16.58bn channel. Today these are all Rule 144A: **no prospectus, no offering memorandum, no ratings letter on EDGAR anywhere.** Everything the project has on pricing comes from Item 7.01/8.01 exhibits that are furnished, not filed, and not audited.
6. **The spread question is closable today without any rule change: TRACE disseminates 144A corporate bond transactions.** That is the dataset that settles "halves the financing cost." It is public and it is not on EDGAR. Rating rationales are not closable through filings at all — Hut 8 discloses six words ("rated Baa2 by Moody's Ratings") and **discloses no rating whatsoever for the $3.25bn River Bend notes, whose pricing release is headlined "Investment-Grade."** That is the most quotable disclosure failure in the pass.

## 4. THE SIZE TEST

| Measure | Level | vs $27,033bn runnable | vs $2,006bn bank→NBFI | vs $3.1trn NFC capex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guarantee ceiling, live | $86.2bn | 0.32% | 4.3% | 2.8% |
| Guarantee ceiling, contracted | $228.3bn | 0.84% | 11.4% | 7.4% |
| **Leases not yet commenced** | **$1,122.9bn** | **4.2%** | **56%** | **36%** |

**Answer: the guarantee layer is a $50bn-class phenomenon and the essay it supports on its own is a small one. But we are in the $500bn essay — the $500bn is not the guarantee, it is the lease.**

Three qualifiers on the small number, in decreasing strength:

- **The ratio, not the level, is the object.** $228.3bn of contracted guarantee sits behind $1,122.9bn of unrecorded lease obligation — about 4.9:1, future against future [C-116: this line set the leases against the $86.2bn *live* figure]. The guarantee is the visible exception; the lease covenant is the ordinary case and is several times bigger.
- **The population is 90% invisible.** $86.2bn disclosed, $8.183bn traceable to a named beneficiary. At Hut 8's River Bend ratio ($7.0bn of base-term contract value supporting $3.25bn of notes, ~2.2x), Alphabet's $43.785bn of notional is consistent with roughly $20bn of backstopped project debt against $8.183bn identified — **flagged explicitly as my computation from a single deal's disclosed schedule, not a disclosure**, and with the competing explanation stated: the notional may also cover leases carrying no bond financing at all, which collapses the implied figure.
- **The trajectory.** Alphabet $0 → $16.94bn → $43.785bn in eighteen months. NVIDIA: zero occurrences of "residual value" in any periodic filing, then $105bn in a single 8-K five days before this pass. Meta ran its Louisiana template a second time at El Paso within nine months. Real, but note this is **one filer replicating its own structure and one instrument scaling — not independent corroboration**, and extrapolating it is not a finding.

## 5. WHERE NEXT

**The essay is neither "hidden systemic layer" nor "nothing here." It is a third thing, and the negative results are load-bearing content, not apologies:** a small, fully disclosed, fast-growing, untagged, asymmetrically-priced guarantee layer sitting on top of a trillion dollars of executed, non-cancellable, unrecorded lease obligation that six of six filers disclose and zero of six tag. The interesting quantity is the 13:1 ratio, and no filer discloses it because no filer is asked to.

**What survives as publishable, verified, and new:**
- $1,122.9bn on one clean basis, 6/6 disclosure, 0/6 XBRL tagging — with tagging absence as the *mechanical explanation* for confected aggregates.
- $1.060bn of beneficiary-disclosed price for a service the guarantor reports at nil.
- 6.3bp: a Google backstop prices within six basis points of a direct AA- covenant, same sponsor, 38 days apart.
- AA- tenant behind Baa2 project paper — seven notches of leakage; and a CoreWeave-tenant project bond pricing 25bp *wider* than CoreWeave's own unsecured.
- Three refutations: the halving claim (max 38%), the NVIDIA-Hut 8 tenancy, and the ~100bp-over-Nvidia benchmark.
- The $662bn and $970bn ghosts: nine filers, two independent lenses, no reproduction on any basis at any vintage. Closest near-miss is Amazon's $650.0bn total commitments, ~2% below $662bn — recorded so nobody mistakes it for the source. **The negative is now well-supported and should be stated as a finding.**

**Independence audit, since it is a standing rule.** Genuinely independent: Meta vs Alphabet as guarantors (different standards, different words, different structures); and — uniquely and most importantly — **Alphabet-as-guarantor vs TeraWulf/Cipher/Hut 8-as-beneficiary, which are opposite sides of the same contracts, disclosed by four unrelated filers under different standards, none citing any other.** That is the strongest evidentiary structure in the project. Not independent: all six filers' not-yet-commenced sentences (one ASC 842 convention observed six times); NVIDIA's $3.5bn (three appearances, one fact); Meta Louisiana/El Paso (one filer, twice); TeraWulf/Cipher warrants (one guarantor's standard terms, twice).

**Do next, in order:**
1. **The NVIDIA Q2 FY2027 10-Q — days away and worth more than any further breadth pass.** Q1 was filed 24 days after quarter end; Q2 ended ~2026-07-26. It will put the $105bn into an audited footnote with a stated measurement basis (ASC 460? 815? 842? — NVIDIA has not said), file the form of the guaranty agreements as an exhibit, and re-measure the $3.5bn. This converts a press-adjacent 8-K number into the largest single guarantee disclosure in the dataset, 2.3x Meta's.
2. **Close the 90% population gap.** EDGAR full-text sweep for "backstop" plus Google, and for Fluidstack as tenant, across all filers and all forms. Cheap, mechanical, and it directly attacks the weakest number in this synthesis.
3. **TRACE 144A new-issue spreads.** The one dataset that settles the halving question. Public, available, off EDGAR, not yet used.
4. **The lessor side.** Meta's Louisiana Venture and SB Energy Corp are the two largest instances and neither is known to file. Meta discloses none of the Venture's debt, coupon or rating — the entire Hut 8 mechanism at the biggest instance is unobserved. If the Venture files anything, that hole closes.
5. **Do not add hyperscalers.** Six filers gave 6/6 determinate coverage; Apple, Broadcom, AMD and Tesla will return nil. Breadth is exhausted.

**On the gate.** The reviewer's gate was: sum the disclosed footnotes before moving to securities-based lending, NAV loans, portfolio margin or internalised synthetic prime. The gate is passed — but **passing it argues against walking through it.** The disclosed guarantee layer turned out to be small *and* fully disclosed *and* two-sided-verifiable. That is weak evidence for a large undisclosed layer sitting next to it, not strong evidence. The honest recommendation is to write the trillion-dollar unrecorded-lease finding and the 13:1 ratio, publish the three refutations, and treat the adjacent-market hunt as a separate hypothesis requiring its own motivation rather than as this argument's next step.
